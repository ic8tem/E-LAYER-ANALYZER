TITLE|Technical report on E-LAYER ANALYZER
SUB|The choices that led to the creation of the program - by Costantino IC8TEM - version 0.4.17, November 2026. Numbers in square brackets refer to the SOURCES tab.
H1|1. Introduction
P|E-LAYER ANALYZER arises from a precise need of the VHF operator: to have, in a single instrument, everything required to interpret the propagation modes that involve the E layer of the ionosphere. Until now sporadic-E, F.A.I., meteor scatter and radio aurora were studied with different programs, often written for a single operating system, sometimes no longer maintained, each with its own geometric model and its own conventions. This report sets out the reasons for the choices made, distinguishing what derives from the literature, what derives from the author's on-air experience and what is a choice of the program, still to be confirmed through use.
H2|1.1 Objectives
LI|To bring together in a single program the analysis of sporadic-E, including multi-hop and extra-continental, of F.A.I., of meteor scatter and of radio aurora.
LI|To work on real data: DX cluster spots in real time and the ADIF logs of the stations.
LI|To make every calculation verifiable: formulas and sources set out step by step, together with real cases for comparison.
LI|To run wherever the operator keeps his station computer, from the Windows PC to the Raspberry Pi.
H2|1.2 Method
P|The work proceeded by successive trials: each tab was first sketched out, then checked against documented cases, then corrected according to the indications of the author, the fruit of years of VHF activity from Capri (JN70CN). The rules that resulted are declared as the author's rules in the summary of the calculation choices, so that the reader can distinguish them from the results of the literature.
H1|2. Architecture of the program
H2|2.1 One platform for all systems
TAG|SCELTA|The program is written in C# on .NET 8 with the Avalonia graphical interface. It was preferred to Windows-bound solutions because a single code base produces the packages for 64- and 32-bit Windows, Linux, Raspberry Pi (ARM64) and Mac, with the same graphics and the same functions; compilation and packages are produced automatically, with test screenshots of every tab in several colour schemes and languages.
TAG|SCELTA|All data remain in the data folder next to the program, and no file is written to AppData or to system folders: the program is moved by copying a folder, is reset by deleting a file, and the station remains master of its own data. For the same reason the LoTW password is never saved.
H2|2.2 A family of programs
P|E-LAYER ANALYZER shares graphics, colour schemes and conventions with YA-MS and YAEC, the author's other programs. YA-MS, devoted to meteor scatter, is contained in its entirety in the METEOR SCATTER tab: an alignment script turns it from a window into a panel without altering its calculations, so that every new version of YA-MS enters E-LAYER ANALYZER without being rewritten. Where the two programs have functions in common, such as spot collection, there is a single rule: inside E-LAYER ANALYZER the YA-MS data collection receives the lines of the DATA COLLECTION telnet sessions, avoiding duplicate connections with the same callsign.
H2|2.3 The interface
TAG|SCELTA|Every analysis tab devotes at least 65% of the window to the map and 35% to the data and calculations: propagation is a geographical fact, and the map must remain the centre of the reading. The projections are chosen for their function: for sporadic-E azimuthal equidistant centred on the station (true distances and azimuths, circles of equal distance) or Mercator, in both cases with the IK3XTV distance circles; Mercator with a free centre for 50 MHz multi-hop on a world scale; equirectangular for F.A.I. and aurora, where the locator grid and the field lines are what matter.
TAG|SCELTA|All texts, documents and the wiki are translated: 36 languages: all those of YAEC plus Danish and Tagalog, and from version 0.4.4 also Brazilian Portuguese, Traditional Chinese, Polish, Czech, Romanian, Hungarian, Greek and Thai. In the F.A.I. and aurora view the locus of the scatter points is called Scatter trail in every language, so as to avoid any ambiguity with the meteor trail.
TAG|SCELTA|In version 0.4.4 the two BENCHMARKs become sub-tabs of SPORADIC-E, next to the REAL-TIME live view: the three sporadic-E views are kept together. Next to the UTC time a hidden check with ten NTP servers around the world tells whether the clock is aligned (NTP:OK or NTP:FAILED), because comparing spots from different clusters requires a reliable time.
TAG|SCELTA|From version 0.4.5 the program updates itself: it reports each new version published on GitHub and, with one click, downloads it and installs it over the one in use, without touching settings and collected data. At start-up it also checks that no program file is missing and, if any is, reports it and downloads it again.
H1|3. The physical choices
H2|3.1 Common geometry
TAG|FONTE|The Earth is a sphere of radius 6372.7955 km, as in the article on F.A.I. [1] and in YA-MS [5]; the elevation towards a point of the layer and the secant law are those of the classical literature. The choice of the sphere rather than the ellipsoid is consistent with the precision of 6-character locators, which is a few kilometres.
TAG|SCELTA|For F.A.I. and aurora, tropospheric refraction is applied with the equivalent Earth radius of 4/3, which shifts the radio horizon by a few tens of kilometres; for sporadic-E, where the distances between the stations are what matters most, the geometry remains that of the true sphere.
H2|3.2 Sporadic-E
TAG|FONTE|The number of hops follows the table by Flavio Egano IK3XTV, published in the study «Propagazione sui 6 metri - Caratteristiche generali» (Propagation on 6 metres - General characteristics; Amateur Radio Propagation Studies, doc. n. 10.36.43, April 2003) [3]: single hop up to 2200-2400 km, double up to 4400-5000 km, triple up to 6900 km. It is an empirical table, derived from real contacts on 50 MHz, and for this reason it was preferred to a purely geometric calculation, which would give theoretical ranges wider than those observed. IK3XTV links the hop length to the height of the Es clouds, about 100 km, which limits the single hop to around 2000 km [29], and to skip shortening, which indicates the rise of the MUF: a skip of 400-500 km on 28 MHz for a MUF reaching 50 MHz, a skip of 500-700 km on 50 MHz for a MUF supporting 144 MHz.

TAG|FONTE|For multi-hop the program places, alongside the hypothesis of equal hops, that of the short first hop, within 1200 km of one's own QTH, which stems from the author's experience [4]. The two hypotheses are compared in the BENCHMARK tabs with the clouds seen on single hop in the same hour: it is the most honest way of letting the data decide.
TAG|SCELTA|The foEs is estimated with the secant law and is always a minimum threshold: a spot says that the frequency got through, not how high the critical frequency was. Clouds are gathered on the 4-character Maidenhead squares, the scale at which operators read and compare propagation.
TAG|FONTE|Sporadic-E does not apply to 432 MHz: its MUF reaches about 150-200 MHz [22], [23] and the ARRL records list Es contacts only on 50 and 144 MHz [24]. The program states this explicitly, because some generic sources, including ones produced by artificial intelligence, claim the opposite.
TAG|FONTE|Two statistical warnings, approved by the author, accompany sporadic-E without changing the calculations: Kp above 3 (IK3XTV-IZ3ESV [51]), which on the author's 50 MHz log does not reduce the frequency of Es and therefore remains only a warning; a 50 MHz QSO beyond 11000 km is flagged as a statistical note (EI7GL [56]).
TAG|SCELTA|Multi-hop on 28 and 50 MHz occurs mostly east-west (Takata [60]) and is announced by active clouds on other continents (EI7GL [58]): this is why the program looks for precursors along transverse paths from the user's station, with the IK3XTV hops and always with the author's short first hop. Paths that cross the magnetic equator but exceed pure TEP (ZS6 - OH, ZD7 - GM [59]) are shown in electric blue as Es coupling into TEP, because the spot cannot tell them apart.
H2|3.3 F.A.I. and aurora
TAG|FONTE|F.A.I. is based on the 2011 article by IC8FAX and IC8TEM [1] and on the page by IC8FAX [2]: scatter occurs where the ray meets the field-aligned irregularities at an angle such as to send it back towards the correspondent. For a pair of stations the correct condition is not mere perpendicularity to the field, but the bistatic Bragg condition (the reflection cone), which the program applies.
TAG|RILIEVO|The magnetic field is calculated with the international IGRF-14 model [16] up to degree 13, verified to within 1 nT against a reference implementation. With the simple dipole the IC8FAX-G4LOH contact turned out to be impossible; with IGRF-14 the scatter point falls 28 km from Brussels, one of the documented scatter points. It was this check that decided the adoption of the complete model.
TAG|SCELTA|The two views, Scatter trail and Isoflection, take up the graphics of Aurora QTF, AuroReflex and SE-PROP by K9SE [9], [11]: the first proposes the most probable point, the second lets the operator check any point chosen with a click. If the model finds no solution, the program says so with the message Calculation not possible and shows no partial results, which would be misleading.
TAG|FONTE|For aurora the program adds the NOAA data (OVATION oval, Kp, solar wind) [15], used as an indicator of the presence of the irregularities, and the criteria of the literature on radio aurora [12]-[14] for the recommended beam heading of both stations.
TAG|FONTE|In version 0.4.4 aurora relies on the sources approved by the author: Lange-Hesse [12] for the 110 km height, perpendicularity within ±3° and the preferred window of 370-1100 km from the stations, which from version 0.4.5 moves with the IGRF-14 inclination of each station; G3WPO [14] and G0ISW [33] for the beam heading between north and east, distances and Doppler; K7CW [34] for digital modes; Newton [48] for maximum distances, around 1000 km north-south and 2000 km east-west; the Kyoto Dst index [46] and the Bureau of Meteorology alerts [44] for storms and the southern hemisphere.
TAG|FONTE|Added are the time-of-day weight from Reeve [52] (auroral reflections within about 4 hours of local solar midnight), aurora on 50 MHz in the United States (ARRL [53]), auroral E in the Swedish Arctic (SM2CEW-SM0FOB [54]) and southern aurora on 50 MHz up to about 2000 km (ANVDG [55]).
TAG|SCELTA|The aurora model has a test bench on real QSOs (BENCHMARK AURORA): of the 298 QSOs of EI5FK and PA0O it explains 296; the K8GU case has been discarded because the antenna used is not known.
H2|3.4 Meteor scatter
P|Meteor scatter is entrusted to YA-MS [5], which calculates the efficiency of the showers, reads the flux of the meteor radars and integrates the live observations. The choice of integrating it in its entirety, rather than rewriting part of it, guarantees that the two programs always give the same result.
H1|4. Recognition of the data
H2|4.1 The spots
P|The quality of every analysis depends on the quality of data recognition: if the skip is derived from reliable spots, the other hops can then be reconstructed and the other clouds sought. For this reason the program applies a series of rules, nearly all of them indicated by the author after checking them against real spots.
LI|Spots with <MS>, <AU>, <AUR>, <TR>, <AS> or <ACS> belong to other propagation modes and do not enter sporadic-E; those in the frequency windows typical of meteor scatter or with the digital MS modes are meteor scatter, as in YA-MS.
LI|On 144 MHz sporadic-E starts beyond 900 km, and an isolated spot is not drawn until it is confirmed by a second spot on the same cloud or by a short 50 MHz hop nearby: a single spot on 2 m is too often tropo or a locator error.
LI|Towards continental Africa and South America, when the midpoint falls in the ±20° band of magnetic inclination, the contact is trans-equatorial and not sporadic-E, unless explicitly declared otherwise.
LI|Missing or imprecise locators are taken from the CALL3 database; the program learns new locators only from the complete form LOC<mode>LOC, which leaves no doubt as to which locator belongs to which station.
LI|The local filter discards cluster lines outside the range 27990 kHz - 145 MHz, and every time the program is closed the spot database is emptied: each session starts again from fresh data.
H2|4.2 The logs
P|The two BENCHMARK tabs apply the same criteria to the ADIF logs: transmitting position from the MY_GRIDSQUARE of each QSO, receiving positions from the GRIDSQUARE values, exclusion of modes declared as different and of trans-equatorial contacts. In BENCHMARK 50 MHz multi-hop is treated beyond 3100 km, a threshold indicated by the author that reliably separates multi-hop contacts from long single hops.
H1|5. Verifications
TAG|RILIEVO|F.A.I. IC8FAX-F6DRO: scatter point JN44GH, deviation 0.97°. IC8FAX-G4LOH: JN49FR, deviation 0.88°, 28 km from Brussels. IC8FAX-FA1MXE with the scatter point at Geneva: deviation 3.82°, judged not satisfactory, consistent with the fact that this contact requires another point.
TAG|RILIEVO|F.A.I. DL5MCG-SV1DH (1987 test via Budapest, DL5MCG [6]): Budapest is on the trail with deviation 2.16°; the points below 1° begin 35 km from Budapest.
TAG|RILIEVO|Aurora IO70VJ-JO59BC with refraction: scatter point JP01AK, off the Shetland Islands, deviation 0.97°, QTF 11° and 299°, consistent with the auroral beam headings observed towards the north.
TAG|RILIEVO|On the author's 50 MHz log (9876 QSOs from 2017 to 2026) the program recognises 1738 QSOs beyond 2400 km on 386 days, and the trans-equatorial rule assigns 62 of them to trans-equatorial propagation, nearly all towards South Africa, Brazil and East Africa.
H1|6. Limitations and developments
LI|Stations are placed at the centre of the locator square: with 4 characters the error reaches about 80 km.
LI|The foEs is a minimum threshold and the secant law overestimates the foEs required at VHF; the figure should be read as a relative comparison between clouds.
LI|The geographical boxes for Africa and South America are approximate; borderline cases can be corrected with the operators' indications.
LI|Planned developments: comparison of two logs from different stations in all the multi-hop analyses, ever finer recognition of the spots, systematic verification of the rules over whole seasons.
H1|7. Conclusions
P|E-LAYER ANALYZER does not replace the operator's experience: it makes it measurable. Every choice of the program is declared, every formula has its source, every rule born of experience is indicated as such and can be put to the test on the logs. It is in this balance between literature, on-air experience and verification against the data that the program finds its raison d'être.
