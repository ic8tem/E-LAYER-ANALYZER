TITLE|Chart of sporadic-E hops on 50 MHz
SUB|From 500 to 12000 km, in steps of 100 km - by Costantino IC8TEM - version 0.6.5, November 2026. The numbers in square brackets refer to the SOURCES tab.
H1|1. How to read it
P|For each distance the chart gives how many hops the program counts (rule of section 3.1 of the technical document: 1 hop up to 2400 km, 2 up to 5000 km, 3 up to 6900 km, then the distance divided by 2300 km rounded up), the typical hop scheme according to the sources and the author's experience, the length of the hops with the foEs each one needs on 50 MHz, and the sources the assignment comes from.
LI|Required foEs: secant law [23] with the cloud at a height of 110 km, foEs = 50.2 MHz / sec φ, where φ is the angle of incidence on the cloud for that hop. At 110 km the longest possible hop is about 2351 km (elevation 0°); longer hops need higher clouds.
LI|Up to 2400 km: single hop. Short hops (500-1200 km) need a high foEs (21 MHz at 500 km, 11.3 MHz at 1200 km) and are rarer; the most frequent hop is between 1500 and 2000 km.
LI|Between 2400 and 2800 km: transition zone. The single hop does not reach, the double hop needs two short hops with a high foEs: rare contacts.
LI|From 2800 to about 4700 km: double hop with equal hops, as in the method ITU-R P.534-6 [76]. For the author it is the typical case, for example from Europe towards Kazakhstan (22/11/2026); the 4700 km limit is that of two hops of 2350 km, the longest hop with the cloud at a height of 110 km.
LI|Beyond about 4700 km, towards the other continents: chain with short hops at the ends [4]. The chart shows the short hops at the typical length of 950 km (peak of the short hop of DF5AI [71]) and divides the rest into middle hops of up to 2200 km. The number of hops is therefore usually higher than that of the program's rule, which counts equal hops.
LI|On north-south paths that cross the magnetic equator, beyond about 5000 km, propagation is usually transequatorial (TEP, [49] and [66]) and not sporadic-E: the chart applies to sporadic-E.
