TITLE|How E-LAYER ANALYZER calculates
SUB|Summary of the calculation choices - by Costantino IC8TEM - version 0.4.18, November 2026. Numbers in square brackets refer to the SOURCES tab.
H1|1. Introduction
H2|1.1 Purpose
P|E-LAYER ANALYZER analyses the VHF propagation modes involving the E layer: sporadic-E (including extra-continental multi-hop), F.A.I. (field-aligned irregularities), meteor scatter (with the YA-MS program) and radio aurora. This document describes each calculation and where it comes from.
H2|1.2 Labels
TAG|FONTE|the step follows a cited source.
TAG|RILIEVO|check made on the program or comparison with a real case.
TAG|SCELTA|choice made by the program, to be confirmed by on-air experience.
H1|2. Common geometry
H2|2.1 Earth, locator, great circle
TAG|FONTE|Spherical Earth with radius r = 6372.7955 km, as in the F.A.I. article [1] and in YA-MS [5].
TAG|SCELTA|Stations are placed at the centre of the locator square (4, 6 or 8 characters). Distance, azimuth, intermediate points and circles use the great-circle formulas:
EQ|d = 2r · arcsin √[ sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ₁ · cos φ₂ · sin²(Δλ/2) ]
EQ|Az = atan2( sin Δλ · cos φ₂ ,  cos φ₁ · sin φ₂ − sin φ₁ · cos φ₂ · cos Δλ )
H2|2.2 Elevation towards a point in the E layer
TAG|FONTE|Formula (2) of the F.A.I. article [1], with x = d / r, H height of the point, h height of the station. The program uses the equivalent two-argument form, exact on the spherical Earth:
EQ|e = atan2( (r + H) · cos x − (r + h) ,  (r + H) · sin x )
TAG|RILIEVO|With H = 110 km the program gives 35.3° at 150 km, like IW1AZJ (35°) on IC8FAX's page [2]; at 800 km it gives 4.2° against the 6° stated for IC8CQF (the distance on the page is approximate: 6° corresponds to about 680 km).
H2|2.3 Hop in the layer and secant law
P|For a ray leaving at elevation e and meeting the layer at height H, the angle of incidence φ (from the vertical) and the ground distance of the reflection point are:
EQ|sin φ = r · cos e / (r + H)        d_rifl = r · (90° − e − φ) [in radians]
EQ|MUF = foEs · sec φ        →        foEs ≥ f / sec φ
TAG|SCELTA|The foEs given by the program is a minimum threshold: the spot only says that frequency f got through. The sec φ factor takes the Earth's curvature into account.
TAG|SCELTA|Sporadic-E height and refraction, correction approved by the author: without refraction, with the layer at 100-110 km, the longest possible single hop is 2243-2351 km at 0° elevation, so the 2400 km of the IK3XTV table [3] would fall below the horizon. The default height goes from 105 to 110 km (within the 95-120 km of Pocock [35] and Dzekevich [37]) and for the elevation and the range of the hops the equivalent Earth radius of 4/3 (tropospheric refraction) is used, as already for F.A.I. and aurora; the default minimum elevation goes from 2° to 1°. The single hop thus reaches about 2440 km (2720 km at 0° elevation). The secant law is still computed on the true geometry, a conservative choice: sec φ is 5.38 at 2000 km and 5.45 at 2400 km.
TAG|RILIEVO|The experience reported by IK3XTV [3] indicates foEs of 12-15 MHz for 144 MHz: the pure secant law gives higher values (about 25-28 MHz over 2000 km). The figure should be read as a relative comparison between clouds, not as an absolute value.
H1|3. Sporadic-E
TAG|SCELTA|Since version 0.4.4 the SPORADIC-E tab has three sub-tabs: REAL-TIME (the live spots, sections 3.1-3.7), BENCHMARK Es (3.8) and BENCHMARK 50 MHz (3.9).
H2|3.1 Number of hops
P|The sporadic-E hop lengths on 50 MHz follow the work of Flavio Egano IK3XTV, «Propagazione sui 6 metri - Caratteristiche generali» (Propagation on 6 metres - General characteristics; Amateur Radio Propagation Studies, doc. n. 10.36.43, April 2003) [3], which gives the indicative distances of Es contacts on the 6-metre band, derived from real contacts. In the study on 10 metres [29] IK3XTV recalls that Es clouds lie at a height of about 100 km, and that this limits the single hop to around 2000 km: double hops are possible but rarer. The table, reproduced below, is the basis of the number of hops, of the rings drawn on the maps and of the calculations of the two BENCHMARKs.
TAB|Hop;Minimum distance;Optimum distance;Maximum distance
TAB|Single hop;500-650 km;1500-2000 km;2200-2400 km
TAB|Double hop;2800-3000 km;3200-4000 km;4400-5000 km
TAB|Triple hop;-;4800-6400 km;6900 km
TAG|FONTE|IK3XTV [3], [29] also explains how to read skip shortening: a sudden shortening of the skip on 28 MHz (400-500 km; contacts under 400 km) indicates that the sporadic-E MUF has reached 50 MHz, and a shortening of the skip on 50 MHz to 500-700 km indicates a MUF able to support propagation on 144 MHz. A foEs of 7.2 MHz supports 10 metres; with a foEs of 12-14 MHz the layer supports propagation up to the upper VHF. Es is very selective (even 20-30 km between two stations can make a difference), openings last from a few minutes to several hours, with a peak from May to July, from 9 to 12 and above all from 17 to 20 local time.
TAG|SCELTA|The number of hops n is taken from the table: n = 1 up to 2400 km, n = 2 up to 5000 km, n = 3 up to 6900 km, beyond that n = D / 2300 km rounded up. Below 500 km the spot is not considered sporadic-E. Between 2400 and 2800 km the program flags the "transition zone".
TAG|SCELTA|Chordal hop, approved by the author as a possible mechanism: a rippled sporadic-E layer can carry the signal on from one cloud to the next without touching the ground (Harrison VK2ZRH [40], 50 and 144 MHz in Australia; EI7GL [41] considers it for the 144 MHz openings of more than 3100 km between Australia and New Zealand). The program mentions it as an alternative to the double hop, but the hops are always computed with the table of IK3XTV [3].
H2|3.2 First hop of both stations
TAG|SCELTA|Equal-hop hypothesis: the reflection points lie along the great circle at fractions (2k − 1) / (2n) of the path. The spotter's first hop is point k = 1 (orange), the DX's first hop is point k = n (purple); with a single hop they coincide at the midpoint (yellow).
TAG|FONTE|Short first hop hypothesis: in multi-hop, the author (IC8TEM) has experience of a short first hop within 1200 km of his QTH [4]. The program draws (open circles) the reflection point halfway along each station's short hop; the length of the short hop is chosen in the tab (1200 km by default).
H2|3.3 Es clouds on Maidenhead squares
TAG|SCELTA|The reflection points (midpoint for a single hop, first hops for multi-hop) are collected in 4-character Maidenhead squares (2° × 1°). For each square the highest minimum foEs and the highest frequency of the spots falling in it are kept. The colour follows the frequency (author's rule): green up to 29 MHz, yellow up to 51 MHz, orange up to 71 MHz, red up to 145 MHz.
P|For the user's own station the program computes the MUF through each cloud at distance d, with a hop of length 2d, only if the cloud is within single-hop range:
EQ|MUF_mia = foEs_nube · sec φ(2d)
TAG|SCELTA|"Es clouds" table: in red the clouds that would give MUF ≥ 144 MHz, in orange ≥ 70 MHz, in yellow ≥ 50 MHz.
P|From the foEs of the square the probable MUF is estimated on the longest hop the cloud can sustain, that is the maximum single hop in IK3XTV's table (2400 km):
EQ|MUF_prob = foEs_cloud · sec φ(2400 km)
TAG|SCELTA|If the probable MUF reaches a frequency class higher than that of the spots (for example spots on 50 MHz with a probable MUF above 51 MHz), the square flashes in the colour of the class reached: it is the signal that the same cloud can open a higher band. For 144 MHz, by the author's choice, the secant does not apply (it would require a 50 MHz hop of about 360 km, while IK3XTV [3] and Pocock [35] speak of 500-700 km): the square blinks red only if the cloud gives a 50 MHz single hop between the minimum sporadic-E distance (450 km) and 500 km, that is 450 km with a tolerance of 50 km.
TAG|FONTE|This is the principle stated by IK3XTV [3], [29]: skip shortening indicates a rising MUF (skip of 400-500 km on 28 MHz for a MUF reaching 50 MHz, skip of 500-700 km on 50 MHz for a MUF supporting 144 MHz). The same method is described by Jacobs and Leinwoll [30]: the MUF is derived from the minimum skip distance on 28 MHz, and a skip under 500 miles (about 800 km) on 28 MHz indicates that 50 MHz will open in the same direction.
H2|3.4 Sporadic-E does not apply to 432 MHz
TAG|FONTE|The MUF of mid-latitude sporadic-E reaches 150 MHz and higher (Whitehead [23]); very dense clouds are needed to bend grazing rays up to 200 MHz (IK3XTV [22]). The ARRL records [24] list sporadic-E contacts only on 50 and 144 MHz, none on 222 and 432 MHz.
TAG|SCELTA|For this reason the SPORADIC-E tab analyses only 28, 40 (8 m), 50, 70 and 144 MHz: 432 MHz spots are never treated as sporadic-E (some generic sources, including ones produced by artificial intelligence, claim otherwise without documentation). F.A.I. does not reach 432 MHz either (author's rule, 14/11/2026): its bands are 50, 70 and 144 MHz. Between 430 and 440 MHz the local filter lets through only aurora spots.
H2|3.5 Author's rule for 144 MHz
TAG|FONTE|Rule set by the author (IC8TEM) for 144 MHz: the minimum sporadic-E distance is above 900 km (shorter spots are discarded); when only one spot has been received the program draws nothing; if a second 144 MHz spot arrives within 5 minutes and the cloud matches, it draws both; it also draws a 144 MHz spot above 900 km when there is at least one 50 MHz short-hop spot nearby. In the BENCHMARK Es, 144 MHz QSOs under 900 km are excluded.
TAG|SCELTA|To apply the rule the program considers the cloud a "match" when the reflection points of the two spots (midpoint, first hops, short first hop) are at most 300 km apart; by "short hop on 50 MHz" it means a single hop within 1200 km, received within 15 minutes of the 144 MHz spot, with its midpoint within 300 km. The second spot must come from a different pair of stations. The top bar shows how many 144 MHz spots have been discarded or are awaiting confirmation.
TAG|RILIEVO|Comparison with G0ISW [65] (50, 70 and 144 MHz, United Kingdom): the shortest sporadic-E observed is beyond 400 km on 50 and 70 MHz and beyond 1400 km on 144 MHz, with the single hop on 144 MHz between 1400 and 2400 km. The author's thresholds of 450 km on 50 and 70 MHz agree with G0ISW and with IK3XTV [3] (single hop from 500-650 km). On 144 MHz the author's rule starts from 900 km, lower than G0ISW, but asks for confirmation by a second spot or by a short hop on 50 MHz: G0ISW gives a distance-only threshold, the author a wider threshold with a confirmation.
H2|3.6 Author's rule: trans-equatorial propagation
TAG|FONTE|Rule set by the author (IC8TEM): unless the spot declares it (ES in the text or PROP_MODE=ES in the log), for paths towards Africa and South America, if the midpoint of the contact falls in the magnetic inclination band between -15° and +15° around the magnetic equator (WMM 2015 inclination chart [28]; 15° value taken from Cracknell [66]), the contact is not sporadic-E but trans-equatorial (TEP).
TAG|RILIEVO|The threshold is on the magnetic inclination, not on latitude: ±15° of inclination corresponds to about ±7.6° of dip latitude (tan I = 2 tan λ). The 15° value comes from Cracknell [66] (G2AHU, with the observations of ZE2JV, 50 MHz), who places the high-density TEP zones at about 5-15° from the zero-dip line. Cracknell measures in degrees of latitude, while the author applies the 15° to the inclination: the program's band is therefore narrower and closer to the magnetic equator, by the author's choice (14/11/2026), which replaces the former empirical evaluation of ±20° based on the last solar cycle. Kennewell and Wilkinson [49] place the crests of the equatorial anomaly at ±10-20° of magnetic latitude, that is at about ±19-36° of inclination.
TAG|SCELTA|The program recognises continental Africa with Madagascar and South America with approximate geographic boxes; as indicated by the author, the Canary Islands, Madeira, Cape Verde and the Arabian peninsula remain outside the rule. It calculates the midpoint on the great circle and its inclination with IGRF-14 [16]; spots and QSOs recognised in this way do not enter the SPORADIC-E tab or the two BENCHMARK tabs, which show how many there are.
TAG|SCELTA|General worldwide rule, based on the source approved by the author (Kennewell and Wilkinson [49]): in addition to the author's rule for Africa and South America, a 3000-8000 km contact that crosses the magnetic equator (end points with magnetic inclination of opposite sign), with the midpoint in the inclination band set in the filters and the direction within 15° of the magnetic meridian (IGRF-14 declination at the midpoint), is trans-equatorial and not sporadic-E, unless the spot declares ES. The Canary Islands, Madeira, Cape Verde and the Arabian peninsula remain excluded. The rule also applies in the two BENCHMARK tabs.
H2|3.6b Author's rule: 144 MHz towards the Canary Islands, Madeira and the Azores
TAG|SCELTA|Author's rule (IC8TEM): on 144 MHz, if a spot links the mainland to the Canary Islands, Madeira or the Azores beyond a single hop (more than 2400 km), the contact is sporadic E followed by a tropospheric duct over the Atlantic. The spot counts only if two or more spots, within 5 minutes, point to the same Es cloud (midpoints of a 1000-2300 km hop from the mainland station, within 300 km of each other); then the full Es hop, the cloud and the dashed tropo leg to the island are drawn. A single spot stays pending. The rule does not apply to 50 and 70 MHz.
TAG|FONTE|The same mixed mechanism, sporadic E followed by a tropospheric duct over the sea, is the one EI7GL [41] uses to explain the 144 MHz openings of more than 3100 km between Australia and New Zealand; the author infers it without being able to confirm it and also considers chordal hop, possible but rare on 144 MHz.
H2|3.6c Statistical warnings
TAG|FONTE|IK3XTV and IZ3ESV, «Prevedere l'E sporadico» [51]: sporadic E occurs in more than 90% of cases with a quiet geomagnetic field (Kp ≤ 3, Ap ≤ 17), and openings up to 144 MHz are about 5% of the total. When the Kp (NOAA, or N0NBH) exceeds 3, the SPORADIC-E map flags it at the top right as a simple warning: the spots are analysed as always.
TAG|RILIEVO|Check of the warning on the author's 50 MHz log (proposal approved by the author; Capri JN70CN, May-August 2020-2025, QSOs at 500-2300 km, definitive GFZ Kp every 3 hours): 702 three-hour intervals with Es on 314 days. 86% of these intervals have Kp ≤ 3, but in the same season Kp ≤ 3 covers 88% of all intervals: the «90%» of [51] coincides with the share of quiet time. In the daytime intervals (06-21 UTC) Es appears in 17% of cases with Kp 0-2, in 22% with Kp 2-3, in 24% with Kp 3-4 and in 22% with Kp above 4: with Kp > 3 it does not decrease. The literature also disagrees (Tang et al. [64]). The result holds for 50 MHz; for 144 MHz it has not been checked.
TAG|SCELTA|Statistical alert, at the author's request: a 50 MHz QSO beyond 11000 km is flagged in the details as a statistical note, with the reference of EI7GL [56] (HZ1SK-K4MOG, 11460 km, at least six hops). The alert does not change the calculations.
H2|3.6d Es coupling into TEP
TAG|SCELTA|At the author's suggestion (ZS6 - OH, SM, LA cases): a path that crosses the magnetic equator almost along the magnetic meridian (within 15°, Kennewell-Wilkinson [49]) but is longer than the 8000 km of TEP, or asymmetric (midpoint with inclination beyond ±15°, one end much farther from the magnetic equator), is not pure TEP: sporadic E can couple into TEP. The spot cannot tell, so the path stays among the Es spots but is drawn in electric blue with the label TEP, in the REAL-TIME tab and in the two BENCHMARK tabs.
TAG|RILIEVO|ZS6 (KG44) - OH (KP20): 9589 km, midpoint at 17.1°N 26.9°E with inclination 19.1°, deviation from the magnetic meridian 5°; ZS6 - SM 9551 km, ZS6 - LA 9642 km. ZD7BG - GM4FVM (IO85, [59]): 7980 km, midpoint at 19.9°N 4.7°W with inclination 19.7°, magnetic equator at 12.0°N: with 4 equal hops the first two reflection points (46.8°N and 28.9°N) are Es, the others fall in the TEP band. For comparison ZS6 - I (7674 km, inclination at the midpoint -6.2°) remains pure TEP.
TAG|FONTE|GM4FVM [59] attributes the QSO with ZD7BG to a multi-hop Es of about four hops and considers TEP less likely; the coupling into TEP is a hypothesis of a commenter (OZ9QV) and of the program's calculation, which here disagrees with the author of the source.
H2|3.6e Multi-hop alert from precursors
TAG|SCELTA|At the author's request the program looks for multi-hop precursors on 28 and 50 MHz (on 144 MHz only the double hop). The active clouds are derived from the midpoints of single-hop Es spots (400-2400 km) of the last 30 minutes, between any stations worldwide. For each target region (thirty regions on six continents) at 2800-13000 km from the station the great circle is traced and divided into hops according to the IK3XTV table [3]: equal hops up to 2400 km and, always, the hypothesis of a short first hop of 500-1200 km [4], stating whether the cloud of the short first hop is there.
TAG|SCELTA|A hop point is covered if there is an active cloud within 300 km with foEs not lower than that required for the hop (MUF = foEs sec φ); it is unobserved if there are no stations in the spots of the last hour within 1200 km, because in an area without observers the absence of spots is not the absence of Es. ALERT: all points covered; PRECURSOR: only one point is missing and it is unobserved. Only transverse paths are considered, at least 30° from the north-south direction, and those that meet the TEP rule are excluded.
TAG|FONTE|Es structures are elongated east-west, never purely north-south (Takata [60], 50 MHz, Japan); the 50 MHz multi-hop Europe - Guam (11000-12190 km, about 6 hops) was preceded by a week of Europe - Japan openings around 9000 km (EI7GL [58]). Examples for Europe: Japan via Kazakhstan and Russia, Indonesia and northern Australia via the Arabian Peninsula, North America via northern Canada, the Caribbean; the calculation works from any station, for example between Japan and North America.
H2|3.7 Azimuthal map
TAG|SCELTA|The map in the SPORADIC-E tab is chosen between two projections. Azimuthal equidistant, centred on the user's own station: distances from the centre and azimuths are true and the circles around the centre are circles of equal distance; the zoom slider (from 300 to 20000 km, also with the mouse wheel) follows the evolution from single hop to multi-hop. Mercator: the world map, to read long paths together with geography. In both, the IK3XTV distance circles [3] are drawn around the station: 500, 1500-2000 and 2400 km for the single hop, 5000 km for the double and 6900 km for the triple hop.
EQ|ρ = d(centre, P) · k        x = ρ · sin(az)        y = −ρ · cos(az)        k = disc radius in pixels / maximum distance in km
TAG|SCELTA|The dashed rings show the km from the centre with a round step (50, 100, 200, 250, 500, 1000, 2000, 2500 or 5000 km) chosen according to the zoom; the outer circle marks the maximum distance shown, and the mouse cursor gives distance and azimuth from the centre.
H2|3.8 BENCHMARK Es: test bench on an ADIF log
P|The BENCHMARK Es tab loads a whole ADIF log, also from another station. The transmitting position is the MY_GRIDSQUARE of each QSO (the most frequent one goes to the centre of the map, and can be changed by hand); the receiving positions are the GRIDSQUAREs. QSOs on 28, 50, 70 and 144 MHz with a locator are used, excluding those with another PROP_MODE and those under 500 km; the QSOs are divided by day and, within the day, by hour.
TAG|SCELTA|Skip by sector: for each 10° azimuth sector from the centre, the closest QSO marks the edge of the skip zone (orange arc) and the farthest the range (green arc); in between is the sector reached.
TAG|SCELTA|foEs of the selection: on each band the shortest single hop gives the highest estimate, foEs ≥ f / sec φ (2.3); with this foEs the program computes the expected MUF at 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2400 km and the distance beyond which 144 MHz would open.
TAG|SCELTA|Multi-hop: the station's first hop, under both hypotheses (equal hops, 3.2, and short first hop [4]), is compared with the midpoints of the single hops of the same day within ±30 minutes: within 300 km the hypothesis is confirmed by a cloud actually seen. The program counts the confirmations of each hypothesis.
EQ|cloud speed = d(centroid at hour h, centroid at hour h+1) / 1 h
TAG|RILIEVO|The centroid of the single-hop midpoints, hour by hour, shows the movement of the clouds (red line). The calculations are indicative: a QSO tells that the path was open, not exactly where the cloud was.
TAG|SCELTA|At the author's request, in both BENCHMARKs the centre of every hop, where the sporadic-E cloud is presumed to be, is marked with a blurred yellow dot (larger for the selected QSO or the one under the mouse); with the short first hop hypothesis on, the dot also marks its centre. It is a presumed position, not a measured one (see the finding above).
H2|3.9 BENCHMARK 50 MHz: multi-hop on 50 MHz
P|The BENCHMARK 50 MHz tab loads the ADIF log of one station and, optionally (Station 2 box), that of a second station, and shows the 50 MHz multi-hop QSOs, i.e. beyond 3100 km (author's rule; the number of hops remains that of the IK3XTV table [3]) on the chosen day and time range (from… to… UTC), on a Mercator map with a freely chosen centre.
TAG|SCELTA|When the mouse comes near a path (within 8 pixels) the line and icons are enlarged and the 6-character WWL square of the other station appears; with a left click a popup asks whether to show all QSOs in the file, from all days, with the end point within 150 km of that point: Yes = the map is cleared and shows those QSOs, No = the map stays as it is.
TAG|SCELTA|The foEs is optional (check box): it colours the paths by the minimum foEs of the equal hops (2.3) and reports it in the table. The reflection points are those of the equal hops (3.2) and of the short first hop within 1200 km [4]; the transmitting position is the MY_GRIDSQUARE of each QSO.
H1|4. F.A.I. and aurora: field-aligned scatter
H2|4.1 The magnetic field
TAG|FONTE|The field is computed with the IGRF-14 model [16] (spherical harmonics up to degree 13, 2025-2030 coefficients interpolated to the date). K9SE's SE-PROP [9] likewise used a spherical-harmonic model (WMM).
TAG|SCELTA|Field of the year of the QSO in the test benches (proposal approved by the author): BENCHMARK AURORA, BENCHMARK Es and BENCHMARK 50 MHz use the historical IGRF-14 epochs from 1900 to 2030 (definitive DGRF models up to 2020, file data/igrf14_storico.csv), interpolated to the year of each QSO, for the aurora scatter point, the Lange-Hesse window and the trans-equatorial rules. With the date unknown, today's field remains. The rest of the program always uses the field of the current date.
TAG|SCELTA|Automatic update of the model, at the author's request: at start-up and then every 30 days the program looks, without asking anything, for the next generation of IGRF coefficients on the NOAA NCEI website (igrf15coeffs.txt and following, the same format as IAGA); if there is one, it takes the coefficients of the latest epoch and the 5-year forecast with the secular variation, saves them in the data folder and uses them at once. Until then IGRF-14 applies.
TAG|RILIEVO|The calculation agrees with ppigrf within 1 nT. With the simple dipole the IC8FAX - G4LOH check finds no scatter point at all; with IGRF-14 it does: the full model is essential.
TAG|RILIEVO|Aurora IO70VJ - JO59BC (1331 km, height 105 km, 4/3 refraction): most likely scatter point JP01AK (61.4° N 0.1° E, off the Shetlands), deviation 0.97°, QTF 11° (el. 0.5°) from IO70VJ and 299° (el. 7.6°) from JO59BC. Without refraction: IP70RP, deviation 1.86°, QTF 359° and 288°.
H2|4.2 The reflection condition
TAG|FONTE|The signal scattered by a field-aligned irregularity leaves on a cone with its axis along B; stations on supplementary contours can make contact (a1 + a2 = 180° [1], ±c contours of K9SE/K4GFG [2] [9] [10]).
P|For each station X, c(X) is computed: the signed angle between the line of sight X → SP and the plane perpendicular to B at the scatter point SP. The deviation from the reflection condition for the A-B contact is:
EQ|c(X) = arcsin( û(SP − X) · b̂ )        dev = | c(A) + c(B) |
TAG|FONTE|For the echo back to the same station (backscatter) the condition becomes ray perpendicular to B, with a tolerance of ±2-3° (Lange-Hesse [12]); intensity drops by about 3-5 dB per degree of offset (Jackel et al. [13], radar measurement at 933 MHz with aspect angles of 5-11°: in VHF near perpendicularity the value is an extrapolation).
H2|4.3 "Scatter trail" view (A-B pair)
TAG|SCELTA|On a 0.2° grid at height H the program looks for points seen by both stations with elevation ≥ minimum and with dev ≤ 2.5°. The colour classes are 0-1°, 1-1.5°, 1.5-2°, 2-2.5° (Aurora QTF style [11]). The top-right box shows QTF and elevation of A (red) and B (green) towards each point of the trail.
TAG|SCELTA|Most likely scatter point: deviation ≤ 1° and minimum sum of the squared distances (lowest path loss); in aurora, points inside the oval are preferred.
TAG|RILIEVO|Checks requested by the author (IGRF-14, H = 110 km, 4/3 refraction, program results): IC8FAX (JN70CN) - F6DRO (JN03TJ), 1086 km: trail from the Bay of Biscay to the Adriatic along the 44th parallel, most likely scatter point JN44GH (44.3° N 8.6° E, above Genoa), deviation 0.97°, QTF 314° (el. 7.8°) from Capri and 77° (el. 9°) from F6DRO; the trail passes 143 km from Geneva. With the 1987 field the point declared by DL5MCG is therefore at the limit of the tolerance.
TAG|SCELTA|Refraction: for elevations and horizons in the F.A.I. and AURORA views the equivalent Earth radius k · r with k = 4/3 (standard atmosphere) is used by default; it can be turned off with the "Refraction" checkbox. Ground distances and the reflection condition are still computed on the true geometry.
EQ|e = atan2( (k·r + H) · cos(d / k·r) − (k·r + h) ,  (k·r + H) · sin(d / k·r) )
TAG|SCELTA|Calculation not possible: if according to the model no point meets the condition within 2.5° (Scatter trail view) or the reflection point is below the station's horizon (Isoflection view), the program shows the "Calculation not possible" popup and no other result.

P|For the reflection point SP and for every ground point G within the horizon of SP the program computes the sum of the contours; the line S = 0 is the locus of the stations that receive A through SP:

TAG|SCELTA|In the dark-background schemes the classes are coloured bands (red 0°, orange 1°, yellow 2°, green 4°, light blue 8°, blue 16°); in the light schemes they are thick lines (contour lines obtained with the marching squares method), thicker for the levels closer to 0°.

H2|4.5 Aurora
TAG|FONTE|OVATION Prime auroral oval, Kp and solar wind from NOAA SWPC [15], updated every 5 minutes and saved in data/swpc. The 1° grid is smoothed with bilinear interpolation on 0.5° cells.
TAG|SCELTA|Recommended heading, for both stations: along each station's perpendicularity line (echo perpendicular to B) the point with the highest oval probability is chosen. Since version 0.4.4 the height is 110 km and the perpendicularity tolerance ±3°, as in Lange-Hesse [12] (echo at 100-120 km, maximum at about 110 km, perpendicularity within ±2-3°); among the possible points, those inside the Lange-Hesse window of each station are preferred; a point outside the window remains possible but is flagged (for example IO70VJ-JO59BC, with the scatter point in JP01AK about 1200 km from IO70VJ).
TAG|SCELTA|Moving window, from version 0.4.5 (proposal approved by the author). Lange-Hesse [12] derives the 370-1100 km window (perpendicularity within ±3° at 110 km, exact at about 500 and 930 km) on a single line, from 54° geomagnetic latitude northwards on the 93° E geomagnetic meridian, and divides the stations into inclination zones (A 63-66°, B 66-69°, up to E 72-74°): from B towards E a scatter point at about 1100 km is needed. The program moves the window for each station with IGRF-14: it computes the distance at which the ray towards magnetic north (magnetic south in the southern hemisphere) becomes perpendicular to the field within 3° at 110 km, and shifts the window by the difference from the Lange-Hesse reference point (53.47° N 7.73° E, inclination about 69°, zone B); the end of the window does not exceed the radio horizon at 110 km. Examples: Capri about 260-990 km, Dublin 360-1090 km, Oslo 470-1200 km. Without IGRF, or where perpendicularity is not reached (inclination above about 77°, stations under the oval), the fixed window remains. The program's straight-ray geometry does not reproduce Lange-Hesse's two exact points (at 54° it finds perpendicularity from about 280-310 km): this is why the window is shifted and not recalculated from scratch.
TAG|FONTE|Operating rules: point between north and east, starting from about 45°; Doppler up to ~2 kHz at 144 MHz, typical range ~1300 km, maximum ~2000 km (G3WPO [14]). Newton [48]: maximum distances around 1000 km north-south and 2000 km east-west, peak 1.5-2 years after solar maximum.
TAG|FONTE|Lange-Hesse [12], 145 MHz in Europe (text rechecked on 14/11/2026, proposal approved by the author): «with geomagnetic quiet, Kp = 0-2, no contact is possible»; with Kp 4-5 contacts are possible at about 30% of the maximum frequency; the maximum comes with Kp 7 for the northernmost paths and rises up to Kp 9 the further south the path is. The work has no tables: the data by Kp are in the frequency diagrams (figures 3-5), without counts for each single Kp. G0ISW [33], 50 and 144 MHz: aurora with Kp of 5 or more; 50 MHz is favoured, 432 MHz very difficult because of the Doppler; distances 250-1100 km. K7CW [34], 50 MHz in the United States: heading 43° over 337 km; FT8 does not decode, CW/SSB or Q65 with wide submodes are better.
TAG|SCELTA|Aurora probability at the scatter point: unlikely with Kp ≤ 2 (Lange-Hesse [12]); otherwise the NOAA oval [15] at the point decides (likely from 10%, possible from 3% or with Kp ≥ 5, G0ISW [33]). No Kp threshold by latitude estimated by the program is used: the oval already takes latitude into account. The tab also shows the typical QTF between N and NE (0-50°), which becomes S-SE in the southern hemisphere, the deviation from the direct path and advice on Doppler and modes; aurora spots beyond 2000 km are flagged as doubtful (G3WPO [14]).
TAG|SCELTA|Other aurora measurements, approved by the author: the Kyoto Dst index [46], downloaded together with the N0NBH data, raises the probability when there is a storm (possible with Dst ≤ -50 nT, likely with Dst ≤ -100 nT, if the NOAA oval at the point is not zero), because during storms the oval moves south; for the southern hemisphere, with the free Bureau of Meteorology key [44], an aurora alert makes the scatter point likely. The Kyoto AE index [45] and SuperDARN convection [47] open in the browser from the Propagation data sub-tab: the AE digital files stop at October 2026 and SuperDARN offers no file to read.
TAG|FONTE|Author's rule (IC8TEM): spots with AU or AUR in the text, with or without the angle brackets (<AU>, <AUR>) and in upper or lower case, are aurora spots. The AURORA tab draws them on the map with the calculation: for each spot of the last 2 hours (at most 30) the trail of scatter points of the spotter-DX pair, with the same model as the Scatter trail view (step 0.5°, deviation within 2.5°, OVATION oval as weight), and the most likely scatter point with QTF and elevation of both stations.
TAG|SCELTA|Auroral E (Au-E) as a mode of its own, at the author's request: spots with AUE, AU-E or AURORAL E in the text, or with the declared mode AUE, are neither aurora nor sporadic E. It is the sporadic E of auroral origin described by G0ISW [33]: without Doppler, on the direct path, after the auroral event. The AURORA tab draws these spots in green on the direct path.
TAG|SCELTA|Time-of-day weight, approved by the author: most auroral reflections fall within about 4 hours of local solar midnight (Reeve [52], 15 MHz observations from a single station in Alaska, without counts; the studies above 30 MHz that Reeve cites put the peaks before midnight, so in VHF the window is a weak indication). The program computes the solar time at the scatter point and states whether it falls inside or outside this window; outside the window aurora remains possible, because during storms contacts are also made in the afternoon and evening (G3WPO [14]).
TAG|FONTE|Other sources cited for aurora: ARRL [53], aurora on 50 MHz in the United States with K index 5-7 and antennas to the north; SM2CEW-SM0FOB [54], auroral E on 50 and 144 MHz in the Swedish Arctic, with weaker and steadier signals on the direct path after the aurora; ANVDG [55], southern aurora in Australia, on 50 MHz up to about 2000 km on east-west paths.
TAG|SCELTA|By the author's decision the AURORA tab also shows the QTF range of both stations along the whole trail (deviation ≤ 1.5°, preferably inside the Lange-Hesse window of both), in addition to the most likely scatter point. The K8GU case [57] has been discarded as a check: the antenna used is not known.
TAG|SCELTA|BENCHMARK AURORA, at the author's request: the AURORA tab has two sub-tabs, REAL-TIME (the live calculation) and BENCHMARK AURORA, a test bench of the model on published real QSOs: 298 QSOs with time and locator, from the logs of EI5FK (IO51RT, Ireland, 2003-2005, 144 and 432 MHz [62]) and from the log map of PA0O (JO33HG, Netherlands [63]). TAG|RILIEVO|Result of BENCHMARK AURORA (version 0.4.18, IGRF-14 field of the year of each QSO): the model finds a scatter point for 297 QSOs out of 298 (100%); for 267 (90%) the scatter point is inside the Lange-Hesse [12] window of both stations, moved with the inclination; only one QSO exceeds 2000 km (OH1NOR, 2101 km). The median computed QTF of EI5FK ranges from 32° to 53° depending on the day (PA0O 69°), i.e. the north-east recommended by G3WPO [14]. With today's field instead of that of 2003-2005 the results hardly change: 296 QSOs explained and 268 inside the window.
TAG|RILIEVO|Declared correction (version 0.4.18, approved by the author): from version 0.4.4 to 0.4.17 the test bench started before the program loaded IGRF-14, so it calculated with the tilted centred dipole and with the fixed window. H2|5.2 Reading the spots
TAG|SCELTA|The line "DX de SPOTTER: FREQ DXCALL comment HHMMZ [LOC]" is read with a regular expression (not by fixed columns). From the comment the program reads locators and mode in the form "JN70CN<ES>JO62QM" (first locator = spotter, second = DX), the propagation mode (ES, FAI, AU, MS, TR, EME, F2, TEP), the S/N ratio and the emission mode. Missing locators are looked up in CALL3 [21]; 6-character locators read in comments are added to data/call3_nuovi.txt.
TAG|SCELTA|Author's rule (IC8TEM): if <MS>, <AU> or <TR> (also TROPO; for aurora also AU or AUR without brackets) appears in the spot text, the spot is not about sporadic-E and the SPORADIC-E tab discards it; it remains useful for meteor scatter and aurora. In the BENCHMARK Es the same role is played by the PROP_MODE field of the log.
TAG|SCELTA|Archive: one CSV file per day in data/spot; the latest spots are kept in memory (at most 50000). With RBN and PSKReporter normally only spots from 28 MHz up are saved.
TAG|FONTE|Author's rule (IC8TEM): every time the program closes, the spot database is emptied, both in memory and in the files in data/spot; each session starts again with no old spots.
TAG|SCELTA|Optional local filter (checkbox in the SETTINGS tab, Spot routing, requested by the author): "DX de" lines with a frequency below 27990.0 kHz or above 145 MHz (maximum frequency set by the author) are dropped; they do not appear in the console and are not stored; between 430 and 440 MHz only aurora spots pass. The YA-MS meteor scatter spot filter still receives all lines and applies its own rules.
TAG|FONTE|YA-MS rules applied here too: a spot is meteor scatter, not sporadic-E, when it falls in a frequency window typical of MS (50190-50300, 70100-70400, 144290-144399 kHz, settable in the METEOR SCATTER tab) without tags of another propagation (<TR> <AS> <ACS> <AU> <ES> <FAI> <F2>), or when it contains an unambiguously MS mode (MSK144, FSK441/FSK41, PSK2K, JTMS, JT6M) or <MS>, case-insensitive. Spots with <AS> or <ACS> (aircraft scatter) are not used for sporadic-E.
TAG|SCELTA|There is only one data collection for the whole program. From version 0.4.18, at the author's request, the spot filters are in the SETTINGS tab, Spot routing sub-tab: a table lists the rules in the order in which they are applied (local filter, auroral E, aurora, F.A.I., meteor scatter modes and windows, isolated FT8 and FT4 on 50 MHz, Q65 and JT65 on 144 MHz, tags of other propagation, trans-equatorial rule, sporadic-E distances by band), with the values in use; next to it are the sporadic-E, F.A.I. and aurora filters and the filter test on a cluster line. TAG|SCELTA|28 and 50 MHz rules (author's rules and IK3XTV): on 50 MHz a contact within 450 km is not sporadic-E; on 28 MHz the minimum distance follows IK3XTV [3], [29], according to whom the skip shortens as the frequency decreases: with the same cloud (same foEs) it is the distance at which the secant law gives, for 28.5 MHz, the same MUF/foEs ratio that the 50 MHz minimum distance gives for 50.2 MHz (about 170 km with 450 km on 50 MHz and the 110 km height). The bandplan is a clue and not an exclusive criterion, so on 50 MHz a spot that falls in the meteor scatter window stays in sporadic-E, unless it reports an MS digital mode (MSK144, FSK441...) or <MS>. YA-MS keeps using the window for its own meteor scatter collection. F.A.I. spots are recognized by the tag <FAI> or fai (in upper, lower or mixed case) and do not enter sporadic-E.
TAG|FONTE|Author's rules (IC8TEM, 14/11/2026). 70 MHz: within 450 km a contact is not sporadic-E, as on 50 MHz. FT8 and FT4 on 50 and 70 MHz go to sporadic-E: if the mode of the spot is known the mode decides, and only if it is not known does the bandplan frequency decide (on 70 MHz FT8 is at 70.154 MHz, inside the 70100-70400 kHz meteor scatter window). On 50 MHz, however, an FT8 or FT4 spot is sporadic-E only if it is part of a cloud, that is if within 3-4 minutes there is another 50 MHz spot with its midpoint within 150 km; an isolated spot goes to meteor scatter. 40 MHz (8 m): only distances within the first hop of the E-layer geometry are accepted. 144 MHz: Q65 and JT65 spots are EME and do not enter sporadic-E, unless <ES> is in the text.
TAG|SCELTA|To apply these rules the program uses 4 minutes and 150 km for the cloud of FT8 and FT4 on 50 MHz (the other spot must come from another pair of stations, in any mode); for 40 MHz a minimum distance derived from the 450 km of 50 MHz with the secant law, as for 28 MHz (about 330 km at a height of 110 km), and a maximum equal to the single hop at 110 km with 1° of elevation and 4/3 refraction (about 2440 km). The YA-MS clause on isolated FT8, in the METEOR SCATTER tab, keeps its values (beyond 1100 km, 3 minutes, midpoint within 300 km, parallel paths within 20°), by the author's choice.

H1|9. Limits
LI|Stations at the centre of the locator square: with 4-character locators the error reaches ~80 km.
LI|The foEs from spots is a minimum threshold, and the secant law overestimates the foEs required at VHF (see 2.3).
LI|The "most likely" scatter point is a criterion of the program: the whole scatter trail is the physical result.
LI|The OVATION oval is a probability model for optical aurora, used as an indicator for radio aurora.
LI|The BENCHMARK Es starts from the locators in the log: with 4-character locators and QSOs of a few hundred km the skip and the estimated foEs are approximate.
