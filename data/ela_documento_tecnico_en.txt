TITLE|How E-LAYER ANALYZER calculates
SUB|Technical document - by Costantino IC8TEM - version 0.4.0, November 2026. Numbers in square brackets refer to the SOURCES tab.
H1|1. Introduction
H2|1.1 Purpose
P|E-LAYER ANALYZER analyses the VHF propagation modes involving the E layer: sporadic-E (including extra-continental multi-hop), F.A.I. (field-aligned irregularities), meteor scatter (with the YA-MS program) and radio aurora. This document describes each calculation and where it comes from.
H2|1.2 Labels
TAG|FONTE|the step follows a cited source.
TAG|RILIEVO|check made on the program or comparison with a real case.
TAG|SCELTA|choice made by the program, to be confirmed by on-air experience.
H1|2. Common geometry
H2|2.1 Earth, locator, great circle
TAG|FONTE|Spherical Earth with radius r = 6372.7955 km, as in the F.A.I. article [1] and in YA-MS [5].
TAG|SCELTA|Stations are placed at the centre of the locator square (4, 6 or 8 characters). Distance, azimuth, intermediate points and circles use the great-circle formulas:
EQ|d = 2r · arcsin √[ sin²(Δφ/2) + cos φ₁ · cos φ₂ · sin²(Δλ/2) ]
EQ|Az = atan2( sin Δλ · cos φ₂ ,  cos φ₁ · sin φ₂ − sin φ₁ · cos φ₂ · cos Δλ )
H2|2.2 Elevation towards a point in the E layer
TAG|FONTE|Formula (2) of the F.A.I. article [1], with x = d / r, H height of the point, h height of the station. The program uses the equivalent two-argument form, exact on the spherical Earth:
EQ|e = atan2( (r + H) · cos x − (r + h) ,  (r + H) · sin x )
TAG|RILIEVO|With H = 110 km the program gives 35.3° at 150 km, like IW1AZJ (35°) on IC8FAX's page [2]; at 800 km it gives 4.2° against the 6° stated for IC8CQF (the distance on the page is approximate: 6° corresponds to about 680 km).
H2|2.3 Hop in the layer and secant law
P|For a ray leaving at elevation e and meeting the layer at height H, the angle of incidence φ (from the vertical) and the ground distance of the reflection point are:
EQ|sin φ = r · cos e / (r + H)        d_rifl = r · (90° − e − φ) [in radians]
EQ|MUF = foEs · sec φ        →        foEs ≥ f / sec φ
TAG|SCELTA|The foEs given by the program is a minimum threshold: the spot only says that frequency f got through. The sec φ factor takes the Earth's curvature into account.
TAG|RILIEVO|The experience reported by IK3XTV [3] indicates foEs of 12-15 MHz for 144 MHz: the pure secant law gives higher values (about 25-28 MHz over 2000 km). The figure should be read as a relative comparison between clouds, not as an absolute value.
H1|3. Sporadic-E
H2|3.1 Number of hops
TAG|FONTE|IK3XTV table [3]: single hop 500-650 / 1500-2000 / 2200-2400 km (minimum / optimum / maximum); double hop 2800-3000 / 3200-4000 / 4400-5000 km; triple hop optimum 4800-6400 km, maximum 6900 km.
TAG|SCELTA|The number of hops n is taken from the table: n = 1 up to 2400 km, n = 2 up to 5000 km, n = 3 up to 6900 km, beyond that n = D / 2300 km rounded up. Below 500 km the spot is not considered sporadic-E. Between 2400 and 2800 km the program flags the "transition zone".
H2|3.2 First hop of both stations
TAG|SCELTA|Equal-hop hypothesis: the reflection points lie along the great circle at fractions (2k − 1) / (2n) of the path. The spotter's first hop is point k = 1 (orange), the DX's first hop is point k = n (purple); with a single hop they coincide at the midpoint (yellow).
TAG|FONTE|Short first hop hypothesis: in multi-hop, the author (IC8TEM) has experience of a short first hop within 1200 km of his QTH [4]. The program draws (open circles) the reflection point halfway along each station's short hop; the length of the short hop is chosen in the tab (1200 km by default).
H2|3.3 Es clouds on Maidenhead squares
TAG|SCELTA|The reflection points (midpoint for a single hop, first hops for multi-hop) are collected in 4-character Maidenhead squares (2° × 1°). For each square the highest minimum foEs among the spots falling in it is kept; the colour goes from blue (< 5 MHz) to red (> 20 MHz).
P|For the user's own station the program computes the MUF through each cloud at distance d, with a hop of length 2d, only if the cloud is within single-hop range:
EQ|MUF_mia = foEs_nube · sec φ(2d)
TAG|SCELTA|"Es clouds" table: in red the clouds that would give MUF ≥ 144 MHz, in orange ≥ 70 MHz, in yellow ≥ 50 MHz.
H2|3.4 Sporadic-E does not apply to 432 MHz
TAG|FONTE|The MUF of mid-latitude sporadic-E reaches 150 MHz and higher (Whitehead [23]); very dense clouds are needed to bend grazing rays up to 200 MHz (IK3XTV [22]). The ARRL records [24] list sporadic-E contacts only on 50 and 144 MHz, none on 222 and 432 MHz.
TAG|SCELTA|For this reason the SPORADIC-E tab analyses only 28, 50, 70 and 144 MHz: 432 MHz spots are never treated as sporadic-E (some generic sources, including ones produced by artificial intelligence, claim otherwise without documentation).
TAG|FONTE|Rule set by the author (IC8TEM) for 144 MHz: the minimum sporadic-E distance is above 900 km (shorter spots are discarded); when only one spot has been received the program draws nothing; if a second 144 MHz spot arrives within 5 minutes and the cloud matches, it draws both; it also draws a 144 MHz spot above 900 km when there is at least one 50 MHz short-hop spot nearby. In the BENCHMARK Es, 144 MHz QSOs under 900 km are excluded.
TAG|SCELTA|To apply the rule the program considers the cloud a "match" when the reflection points of the two spots (midpoint, first hops, short first hop) are at most 300 km apart; by "short hop on 50 MHz" it means a single hop within 1200 km, received within 15 minutes of the 144 MHz spot, with its midpoint within 300 km. The second spot must come from a different pair of stations. The top bar shows how many 144 MHz spots have been discarded or are awaiting confirmation.
H2|3.5 Azimuthal map
TAG|SCELTA|The map in the SPORADIC-E tab is azimuthal equidistant, centred on the user's own station: distances from the centre and azimuths are true, and the circles around the centre are circles of equal distance. A button switches between the single-hop view (fixed radius 2500 km, with the zones of the IK3XTV table [3]: 500, 1500-2000 and 2400 km) and the view with the maximum-distance slider (from 300 to 20000 km, also with the mouse wheel), which adds the limits of the double hop (5000 km) and of the triple hop (6900 km) to follow the evolution of multi-hop. The rectangular map is still available.
EQ|ρ = d(centre, P) · k        x = ρ · sin(az)        y = −ρ · cos(az)        k = disc radius in pixels / maximum distance in km
TAG|SCELTA|The dashed rings show the km from the centre with a round step (50, 100, 200, 250, 500, 1000, 2000, 2500 or 5000 km) chosen according to the zoom; the outer circle marks the maximum distance shown, and the mouse cursor gives distance and azimuth from the centre.
H2|3.6 BENCHMARK Es: test bench on an ADIF log
P|The BENCHMARK Es tab loads a whole ADIF log, also from another station. The transmitting position is the MY_GRIDSQUARE of each QSO (the most frequent one goes to the centre of the map, and can be changed by hand); the receiving positions are the GRIDSQUAREs. QSOs on 28, 50, 70 and 144 MHz with a locator are used, excluding those with another PROP_MODE and those under 500 km; the QSOs are divided by day and, within the day, by hour.
TAG|SCELTA|Skip by sector: for each 10° azimuth sector from the centre, the closest QSO marks the edge of the skip zone (orange arc) and the farthest the range (green arc); in between is the sector reached.
TAG|SCELTA|foEs of the selection: on each band the shortest single hop gives the highest estimate, foEs ≥ f / sec φ (2.3); with this foEs the program computes the expected MUF at 1000, 1500, 2000 and 2400 km and the distance beyond which 144 MHz would open.
TAG|SCELTA|Multi-hop: the station's first hop, under both hypotheses (equal hops, 3.2, and short first hop [4]), is compared with the midpoints of the single hops of the same day within ±30 minutes: within 300 km the hypothesis is confirmed by a cloud actually seen. The program counts the confirmations of each hypothesis.
EQ|cloud speed = d(centroid at hour h, centroid at hour h+1) / 1 h
TAG|RILIEVO|The centroid of the single-hop midpoints, hour by hour, shows the movement of the clouds (red line). The calculations are indicative: a QSO tells that the path was open, not exactly where the cloud was.
H1|4. F.A.I. and aurora: field-aligned scatter
H2|4.1 The magnetic field
TAG|FONTE|The field is computed with the IGRF-14 model [16] (spherical harmonics up to degree 13, 2025-2030 coefficients interpolated to the date). K9SE's SE-PROP [9] likewise used a spherical-harmonic model (WMM).
TAG|RILIEVO|The calculation agrees with ppigrf within 1 nT. With the simple dipole the IC8FAX - G4LOH check finds no scatter point at all; with IGRF-14 it does: the full model is essential.
TAG|RILIEVO|Aurora IO70VJ - JO59BC (1331 km, height 105 km, 4/3 refraction): most likely scatter point JP01AK (61.4° N 0.1° E, off the Shetlands), deviation 0.97°, QTF 11° (el. 0.5°) from IO70VJ and 299° (el. 7.6°) from JO59BC. Without refraction: IP70RP, deviation 1.86°, QTF 359° and 288°.
H2|4.2 The reflection condition
TAG|FONTE|The signal scattered by a field-aligned irregularity leaves on a cone with its axis along B; stations on supplementary contours can make contact (a1 + a2 = 180° [1], ±c contours of K9SE/K4GFG [2] [9] [10]).
P|For each station X, c(X) is computed: the signed angle between the line of sight X → SP and the plane perpendicular to B at the scatter point SP. The deviation from the reflection condition for the A-B contact is:
EQ|c(X) = arcsin( û(SP − X) · b̂ )        dev = | c(A) + c(B) |
TAG|FONTE|For the echo back to the same station (backscatter) the condition becomes ray perpendicular to B, with a tolerance of ±2-3° (Lange-Hesse [12]); intensity drops by about 3-5 dB per degree of offset (Jackel et al. [13]).
H2|4.3 "Scatter trail" view (A-B pair)
TAG|SCELTA|On a 0.2° grid at height H the program looks for points seen by both stations with elevation ≥ minimum and with dev ≤ 2.5°. The colour classes are 0-1°, 1-1.5°, 1.5-2°, 2-2.5° (Aurora QTF style [11]). The top-right box shows QTF and elevation of A (red) and B (green) towards each point of the trail.
TAG|SCELTA|Most likely scatter point: deviation ≤ 1° and minimum sum of the squared distances (lowest path loss); in aurora, points inside the oval are preferred.
TAG|RILIEVO|Checks requested by the author (IGRF-14, H = 110 km, 4/3 refraction, program results): IC8FAX (JN70CN) - F6DRO (JN03TJ), 1086 km: trail from the Bay of Biscay to the Adriatic along the 44th parallel, most likely scatter point JN44GH (44.3° N 8.6° E, above Genoa), deviation 0.97°, QTF 314° (el. 7.8°) from Capri and 77° (el. 9°) from F6DRO; the trail passes 143 km from Geneva. IC8FAX - G4LOH (IO70JC), 1843 km: trail over south-western Germany and Belgium, most likely scatter point JN49FR (49.7° N 8.5° E), deviation 0.88°, QTF 338° (el. 1.9°) from Capri and 87° (el. 3°) from G4LOH; the trail passes 28 km from the known Brussels scatter point. Without refraction the trails get shorter (G4LOH: elevations 0.5° and 2.1°). IC8FAX - FA1MXE (JN05GQ, first F.A.I. QSO in 1990 via Geneva at 330°): the trail passes near Geneva.
TAG|RILIEVO|DL5MCG case [6]: beacon DB0FAI (JN58IC) - Dresden via Sheffield, deviation 0.3° at 110 km.
TAG|SCELTA|Refraction: for elevations and horizons in the F.A.I. and AURORA views the equivalent Earth radius k · r with k = 4/3 (standard atmosphere) is used by default; it can be turned off with the "Refraction" checkbox. Ground distances and the reflection condition are still computed on the true geometry.
EQ|e = atan2( (k·r + H) · cos(d / k·r) − (k·r + h) ,  (k·r + H) · sin(d / k·r) )
TAG|SCELTA|Calculation not possible: if according to the model no point meets the condition within 2.5° (Scatter trail view) or the reflection point is below the station's horizon (Isoflection view), the program shows the "Calculation not possible" popup and no other result.
H2|4.4 "Isoflection" view (isoflection loci): checking the reflection point
TAG|FONTE|This is the typical geometric construction of F.A.I. and aurora for checking whether the chosen reflection point is right (isoflection lines of SE-PROP [9], AuroReflex [11], supplementary contours of K9SE/K4GFG [2] [10]).
P|For the reflection point SP and for every ground point G within the horizon of SP the program computes the sum of the contours; the line S = 0 is the locus of the stations that receive A through SP:
EQ|S(G) = c(A) + c(G)        plotted levels: 0, ±1, ±2, ±4, ±8, ±16°
TAG|SCELTA|In the dark-background schemes the classes are coloured bands (red 0°, orange 1°, yellow 2°, green 4°, light blue 8°, blue 16°); in the light schemes they are thick lines (contour lines obtained with the marching squares method), thicker for the levels closer to 0°.
TAG|SCELTA|The program proposes the most likely scatter point ("Check the recommended point" button), but the user can choose any other point with a left click on the map; a right click moves station B. Verdict on the A-B pair: satisfactory within 1°, acceptable within 2.5° (Lange-Hesse tolerance [12]), not satisfactory beyond. If the point is below the horizon of either station the "Calculation not possible" popup appears.
TAG|SCELTA|At the top: azimuth and elevation of A and B towards the point; green circle = horizon of the stations at the layer height, red circle = area from which the point is visible.
H2|4.5 Aurora
TAG|FONTE|OVATION Prime auroral oval, Kp and solar wind from NOAA SWPC [15], updated every 5 minutes and saved in data/swpc. The 1° grid is smoothed with bilinear interpolation on 0.5° cells.
TAG|SCELTA|Recommended heading, for both stations: along each station's perpendicularity line (echo perpendicular to B, tolerance 1.5°, height 105 km) the point with the highest oval probability is chosen. The 105 km height lies between Jackel's 104 km [13] and Lange-Hesse's 110 km [12].
TAG|FONTE|Operating rules: point between north and east, starting from about 45°; Doppler up to ~2 kHz at 144 MHz, typical range ~1300 km, maximum ~2000 km (G3WPO [14]).
H1|5. Data collection
H2|5.1 Telnet sessions
TAG|SCELTA|Three sessions: DX cluster of your choice, Reverse Beacon Network (7000 CW/RTTY, 7001 FT8) [18], cluster with PSKReporter spots (e.g. DM4X dm4x.ddns.net:8500 [27], HamAlert [19]). Automatic login with the callsign, initial commands separated by semicolons, reconnection after 30 s.
H2|5.2 Reading the spots
TAG|SCELTA|The line "DX de SPOTTER: FREQ DXCALL comment HHMMZ [LOC]" is read with a regular expression (not by fixed columns). From the comment the program reads locators and mode in the form "JN70CN<ES>JO62QM" (first locator = spotter, second = DX), the propagation mode (ES, FAI, AU, MS, TR, EME, F2, TEP), the S/N ratio and the emission mode. Missing locators are looked up in CALL3 [21]; 6-character locators read in comments are added to data/call3_nuovi.txt.
TAG|SCELTA|Author's rule (IC8TEM): if <MS>, <AU> or <TR> (also AUE, AURORA, TROPO) appears in the spot text, the spot is not about sporadic-E and the SPORADIC-E tab discards it; it remains useful for meteor scatter and aurora. In the BENCHMARK Es the same role is played by the PROP_MODE field of the log.
TAG|SCELTA|Archive: one CSV file per day in data/spot; the latest spots are kept in memory (at most 50000). With RBN and PSKReporter normally only spots from 28 MHz up are saved.
TAG|FONTE|Author's rule (IC8TEM): every time the program closes, the spot database is emptied, both in memory and in the files in data/spot; each session starts again with no old spots.
TAG|SCELTA|Optional local filter (checkbox in DATA COLLECTION, requested by the author): "DX de" lines with a frequency below 27990.0 kHz are dropped; they do not appear in the console and are not stored.
TAG|FONTE|YA-MS rule applied here too: spots are meteor scatter, not sporadic-E, when they use unambiguously MS modes (MSK144, FSK441/FSK41, PSK2K, JTMS, JT6M) or have <MS> in the text, case-insensitive.
H1|6. Log ADIF
TAG|FONTE|Port of LoTW Grid Map v2 [25]: the LoTW report (downloaded with username and password, which is never saved) and the log's ADIF files are merged by callsign, band, date and time; confirmations are combined.
TAG|SCELTA|Only bands from 28 MHz up, each as a single band, with no grouping. Colours: orange = worked, blue = confirmed by QSL, green = confirmed via LoTW. Filter on the propagation mode (ADIF PROP_MODE: ES, FAI, AUR/AUE, MS, TR, EME, F2).
H1|7. Colour schemes
TAG|FONTE|The colour schemes are those of YA-MS 1.4.0, which follow YAEC [26]: Light, Night, Black, eight pastels and Meteor trail. The scheme is chosen at the top right and the window is rebuilt at once; in the Isoflection view dark schemes use coloured bands and light schemes thick lines (4.4).
H1|8. Meteor scatter (YA-MS 1.4.0) and languages
TAG|FONTE|The METEOR SCATTER tab contains the complete YA-MS 1.4.0 [5], with its own "Efficiency - explanation" document and sources. The script tools/sync_yams.py copies every new YA-MS version and turns it into a panel without changing its calculations.

TAG|SCELTA|Languages: 28, all those of YAEC plus Danish and Tagalog (Italiano, English, Deutsch, Français, Español, Português, Nederlands, Svenska, Norsk (bokmål), Dansk, Íslenska, Eesti, Latviešu, Lietuvių, Русский, Українська, Türkçe, Afrikaans, العربية, עברית, اردو, हिन्दी, বাংলা, Bahasa Indonesia, Tagalog, 中文 (简体), 日本語, 한국어), for the interface (data/lingue/ela-<code>.txt), the Technical document and the Sources. In the F.A.I./aurora view the trail of scatter points is called "Scatter trail" in every translation. The status bar shows the magnetic model in use (IAGA IGRF-14 [16]).
H1|9. Limits
LI|Stations at the centre of the locator square: with 4-character locators the error reaches ~80 km.
LI|The foEs from spots is a minimum threshold, and the secant law overestimates the foEs required at VHF (see 2.3).
LI|The "most likely" scatter point is a criterion of the program: the whole scatter trail is the physical result.
LI|The OVATION oval is a probability model for optical aurora, used as an indicator for radio aurora.
LI|The BENCHMARK Es starts from the locators in the log: with 4-character locators and QSOs of a few hundred km the skip and the estimated foEs are approximate.
