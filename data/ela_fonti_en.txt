TITLE|E-LAYER ANALYZER sources
SUB|List of the sources used by the program, tab by tab - by Costantino IC8TEM - version 0.4.3, November 2026
H1|Main sources
SRC|1. G. Cerrotta IC8FAX, C. Cerrotta IC8TEM, "IL F.A.I., quello strano fenomeno del dopo E-sporadico. Analisi del modo di propagazione ed esposizione di osservazioni in merito", Capri, May 2011 (in Italian). Cone geometry, a1 + a2 = 180° rule, elevation formula, season and times, Geneva scatter point.
SRC|2. G. Cerrotta IC8FAX, "Field Aligned Irregularities", web page https://qsl.net/ic8fax/page4.html. Reflection cone and supplementary contours, known scatter points (Geneva, Brussels, Budapest, JN92, JN41/42), example elevations (IW1AZJ 35° at ~150 km, IC8CQF 6° at ~800 km), season May to mid-September, 16-22 UTC, squares worked from JN70CN, reference to K9SE's program.
SRC|3. F. Egano IK3XTV, «Propagazione sui 6 metri - Caratteristiche generali» (Propagation on 6 metres - General characteristics), Amateur Radio Propagation Studies, doc. n. 10.36.43, April 2003, https://www.qsl.net/ik3xtv/ARCHIVIO/PROPAGAZIONE%20SUI%206%20METRI.pdf : indicative distances of Es contacts on 6 metres - single hop minimum 500-650 km, optimum 1500-2000 km, maximum 2200-2400 km; double hop minimum 2800-3000 km, optimum 3200-4000 km, maximum 4400-5000 km; triple hop optimum 4800-6400 km, maximum 6900 km; skip shortening on 28 MHz (400-500 km) and on 50 MHz (500-700 km) as an indicator of the MUF; geographical selectivity, duration of openings, season and times. Other articles by IK3XTV at https://www.qsl.net/ik3xtv/ (e.g. "E sporadico alle medie latitudini", Radio Rivista, June 2012; "Prevedere l'E sporadico", with IZ3ESV).
SRC|4. C. Cerrotta IC8TEM, personal experience: in multi-hop the first hop can be short, within 1200 km of his QTH ("short first hop" hypothesis in the SPORADIC-E tab).
SRC|5. C. Cerrotta IC8TEM, G. Sartori IV3GTH, "METEOR-SCATTER. Ovvero come collegare l'Europa in VHF tutto l'anno senza tante pretese" (main source of YA-MS, METEOR SCATTER tab; the YA-MS sources are listed in the program itself).
SRC|22. F. Egano IK3XTV, "Propagazione sui 2 metri" (https://www.qsl.net/ik3xtv/ARCHIVIO/PROPAGAZIONE%20IN%20GAMMA%202%20METRI.pdf): very dense Es clouds are needed to bend grazing rays up to 200 MHz; 144 MHz is reached in about 1 % of the cases of Es usable on HF.
SRC|23. D. Whitehead, "Sporadic E - A Mystery Solved?", QST, October 1997 (https://www.arrl.org/files/file/Technology/tis/info/pdf/9710039.pdf): "The MUF for mid-latitude ES can reach 150 MHz and higher".
SRC|24. ARRL, "World Above 50 MHz Distance Records", update of 19 May 2023 (N. Stearns AA7A), https://www.arrl.org/files/file/WA50-Standings/Distance-Records-19-May-2023-Rev.pdf: sporadic-E records only on 50 and 144 MHz; no Es records on 222 and 432 MHz.
H1|F.A.I.
SRC|6. W. Rauh DL5MCG (with DL4MEA), "FAI & DB0FAI", https://qsl.net/dl5mcg/fai.htm. Height ~110 km, aim at the scatter point and not at the DX station, Dresden-Sheffield examples (beacon DB0FAI JN58IC, 305°) used to verify the calculation.
SRC|7. C. Haldoupis, A. Bourdillon, A. Kamburelis, G. C. Hussey, J. A. Koehler, "50 MHz continuous wave interferometer observations of the unstable mid-latitude E-region ionosphere", Annales Geophysicae 21, 1589-1600, 2003. https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/21/1589/2003/angeo-21-1589-2003.pdf
SRC|8. S. Saito, M. Yamamoto, S. Fukao, M. Marumoto, R. T. Tsunoda, "Radar observations of field-aligned plasma irregularities in the SEEK-2 campaign", Annales Geophysicae 23, 2307-2318, 2005. https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/23/2307/2005/
SRC|9. J. M. Roop K9SE, SE-PROP for Windows 1.0.5 (1995-1999): evaluation of sporadic-E paths and isoflection lines ("isoflection lines") for F.A.I., with the WMM magnetic model. Program supplied by the author of E-LAYER ANALYZER.
SRC|10. F. Kneisel K4GFG, QST, January 1982, and "Ionospheric scatter by Field Aligned Irregularities at 144 MHz", DUBUS 3/86 (cited in [2]).
SRC|11. AuroReflex and Aurora QTF programs (images supplied by the author): graphic model for the curves of deviation from the reflection condition and for the QTF box.
H1|Aurora
SRC|12. G. Lange-Hesse, "V.H.F.-Bistatic-Aurora Communications as a Function of Geomagnetic Activity and Magnetic Latitude", Max-Planck-Institut für Ionosphärenphysik (NASA NTRS 19650029499). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19650029499/downloads/19650029499.pdf
SRC|13. B. J. Jackel, D. R. Moorcroft, K. Schlegel, "Characteristics of very large aspect angle E-region coherent echoes at 933 MHz", Annales Geophysicae 15, 54-62, 1997. https://angeo.copernicus.org/articles/15/54/1997/angeo-15-54-1997.pdf
SRC|14. T. Bailey G3WPO, "Newcomers" column on auroral propagation, Ham Radio Today, November 1983, p. 25. https://worldradiohistory.com/UK/Ham-Radio-Today/80s/HRT-1983-11-Page-0025.pdf
SRC|15. NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center: OVATION Prime auroral oval (services.swpc.noaa.gov/json/ovation_aurora_latest.json), planetary Kp index (products/noaa-planetary-k-index.json), RTSW real-time solar wind (json/rtsw/rtsw_mag_1m.json, rtsw_wind_1m.json); NWS notice SCN 26-21 on the new formats.
H1|Geomagnetic field and cartography
SRC|16. IAGA, International Geomagnetic Reference Field, 14th generation (IGRF-14), doi:10.5281/zenodo.14012302; coefficients read from the IGRF14.shc file distributed with ppigrf (K. M. Laundal, MIT licence), also used to verify the calculation.
SRC|28. NOAA NCEI and BGS, World Magnetic Model 2015, world chart of magnetic inclination (provided by the author): band of ±20° around the magnetic equator used for the trans-equatorial rule (3.6 of the Summary of the calculation choices); in the program the inclination is calculated with IGRF-14 [16].
SRC|17. Natural Earth (public domain), coastlines and borders at 1:50 million scale, www.naturalearthdata.com.
H1|Log ADIF and colour schemes
SRC|25. C. Cerrotta IC8TEM, LoTW Grid Map v2: ADIF reading, LoTW report (lotwreport.adi), colours of worked and confirmed squares (LOG ADIF tab).
SRC|26. C. Cerrotta IC8TEM, YAEC - Yet Another EME Calc: light, blue night, black and pastel colour schemes (Tema class).
H1|Data collection
SRC|18. Reverse Beacon Network, telnet servers (port 7000 CW/RTTY, 7001 FT8): https://www.reversebeacon.net/pages/Telnet+servers+30
SRC|19. HamAlert (spots from PSKReporter, RBN, clusters; telnet hamalert.org:7300): https://hamalert.org/help
SRC|27. DM4X DX cluster with PSKReporter spots: telnet dm4x.ddns.net, port 8500 (address supplied by the author of the program).
SRC|20. ON0DXK DX cluster (CC Cluster, VHF filters): https://www.qsl.net/on0dxk ; HamQTH DX cluster (hamqth.com:7300): https://hamqth.com/dxcluster.php
SRC|21. CALL3.TXT file from JTDX (I. UA3DJY, JTAlert/hamspots.net data, ES1JA's script): callsign -> locator database.
SRC|29. F. Egano IK3XTV, «Propagazione sui 10 metri - Caratteristiche generali» (Propagation on 10 metres - General characteristics), Amateur Radio Propagation Studies, https://www.qsl.net/ik3xtv/ARCHIVIO/PROPAGAZIONE%20SUI%2010%20METRI.pdf : Es clouds at a height of about 100 km, single hop limited to around 2000 km, double hops possible but rarer; skip under 400 km on 28 MHz as an indicator of a MUF above 50 MHz; foEs 7.2 MHz for 10 metres, 12-14 MHz for the upper VHF.
SRC|30. G. Jacobs W3ASK, S. Leinwoll, «VHF Ionospheric Propagation», CQ Magazine, November 1969 (https://wtfda.org/wp-content/uploads/mem/vhf_ionospheric_propagation.pdf): the sporadic-E MUF is derived from the minimum skip distance on 28 MHz; a skip under 500 miles on 28 MHz indicates that 50 MHz will open in the same direction; single Es hop up to about 1300 miles, double up to about 2400 miles.
SRC|31. P. Herrman N0NBH, solar data and VHF conditions (https://www.hamqsl.com/solarxml.php, banner https://www.hamqsl.com/solar101vhf.php; VHF data from DXrobot and Make More Miles on VHF): E-skip Europe and North America, aurora, solar and geomagnetic indices; to be downloaded at most once an hour.
SRC|32. Make More Miles on VHF (https://mmmonvhf.de/ms.php): chart of today's meteor activity (https://mmmonvhf.de/ms/ms.png) with data from the Radio Meteor Observing Bulletin (RMOB), updated every hour.
SRC|33. G0ISW, «VHF DX Resource» (https://www.qsl.net/g0isw/g0iswmain.html): aurora on 50 and 144 MHz (50 MHz favoured, 432 MHz very difficult because of the Doppler); Kp of 5 or more; antennas between north and east; distances 250-1100 km; auroral E develops after the aurora, without Doppler.
SRC|34. K7CW and VE7DAY, PNWVHFS group, 2022 (https://groups.google.com/g/pnwvhfs/c/x7-pBO19WGc): aurora on 50 MHz between the north-western United States and Canada, heading 43°, 337 km; FT8 does not decode, Q65 with wide submodes recommended.
SRC|35. E. Pocock W3EP, «Sporadic-E propagation at VHF: a review of progress and prospects», QST, April 1988 (reprint in UKSMG Six News: https://uksmg.org/content/sporade.htm): 50, 144 and 220 MHz, North America; MUF about 5.3 times the foEs; maximum single hop about 2200 km with the layer at 105 km; with a cloud that brings the MUF to 144 MHz the skip on 50 MHz is about 650 km; Es on 144 MHz is less than a tenth of that on 50 MHz; F.A.I. up to 2000 km on 144 MHz.
SRC|36. P. Dyer WA5IYX, «A Seven Year Study of 50 MHz Sporadic-E Propagation», CQ, August 1972 (https://www.qsl.net/p/pjdyer/cq/cq7208a.htm): 50 MHz, Texas 1964-1970; almost 80% of openings from May to August; peaks at 10-12 and 18-20 local time; single hop up to about 2250 km, double up to about 4000 km; minimum observed distance about 450 km.
SRC|37. J. Dzekevich K1YOW, «Upper-Level Lows and 6-Meter Sporadic E», QST, December 2017 (https://hamsci.org/sites/default/files/publications/2017_QST_Dzekevich_UpperLevelLows.pdf): 50 MHz, North America; Es layers at 90-120 km, 0.5-5 km thick, 10-1000 km wide; correlation with upper-level meteorological lows.
SRC|38. J. Dzekevich K1YOW, «Winter Sporadic-E-Like Propagation on 6 Meters», CQ, November 2020 (abstract: https://hamsci.org/bibcite/reference/173): 50 MHz, winter sporadic E and the jet stream.
SRC|39. C. Luetzelschwab K9LA, «Interesting Propagation on 6 Meters», 6m BBQ 2018 (https://contestuniversity.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/5.-6m-BBQ-K9LA-Austin-2018.pdf): 50 MHz; FT8 decodes with a MUF of at least about 44 MHz, CW with about 45.5 MHz.
SRC|40. R. Harrison VK2ZRH, «A New Model of VHF Sporadic E Propagation», DUBUS 2/2011 (https://vk4ghz.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=17446): 50 and 144 MHz, Australia; with the «chordal hop» model (rippled Es) a foEs of 12-17 MHz is enough for 144 MHz and the MUF almost doubles compared with the classical secant law.
SRC|41. EI7GL, VK-ZL openings on 144 MHz (https://ei7gl.blogspot.com/2019/12/recent-3000km-opening-on-144-mhz.html ; https://ei7gl.blogspot.com/2022/01/3155-km-opening-on-144-mhz-between.html): 144 MHz, Australia and New Zealand; beyond 3100 km the path is explained by sporadic E followed by a tropospheric duct over the sea, not by a double hop.
SRC|42. S. Franke K9AN, J. Taylor K1JT, «The MSK144 Protocol for Meteor-Scatter Communication», QEX, September/October 2017 (https://ARRL.org/files/file/QEX_Next_Issue/SeptOct2017/FrankeTaylor.pdf): 50 and 144 MHz; useful pings above about 70 ms; on 144 MHz pings last about 1/8 as long as on 50 MHz.
SRC|43. J. Taylor K1JT, «WSJT: New Software for VHF Meteor-Scatter Communication», QST, December 2001 (https://mirror.unpad.ac.id/orari/orari-diklat/teknik/jt65/docs/WSJT_QST_Dec2001.pdf): 50 and 144 MHz; distances 800-1800 km; median QSO duration 5 minutes on 6 m and 13 minutes on 2 m.
SRC|44. Bureau of Meteorology, Australian Space Weather Forecasting Centre, Space Weather API (https://sws-data.sws.bom.gov.au/ ; specification: https://sws-data.sws.bom.gov.au/api-docs): Australian K index, Dst, alerts, watches and outlooks of the southern aurora (k_aus, latitude band); POST requests in JSON with a free key.
SRC|45. World Data Center for Geomagnetism, Kyoto, real-time AE index (https://wdc.kugi.kyoto-u.ac.jp/ae_realtime/): auroral electrojet activity; non-commercial use only. The digital files stop at October 2026: the program opens the page in the browser.
SRC|46. World Data Center for Geomagnetism, Kyoto, hourly quicklook Dst index (https://wdc.kugi.kyoto-u.ac.jp/dst_realtime/presentmonth/index.html): intensity of geomagnetic storms; non-commercial use only.
SRC|47. SuperDARN Canada, real-time convection (https://superdarn.ca/realtime-convection): northern-hemisphere convection map with the equatorward boundary (Heppner-Maynard) and the OVATION oval; © SuperDARN Canada, link only.
