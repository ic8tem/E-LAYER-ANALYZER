TITLE|E-LAYER ANALYZER sources
SUB|List of the sources used by the program, tab by tab - by Costantino IC8TEM - version 0.6.5, November 2026
H1|Main sources
SRC|1. G. Cerrotta IC8FAX, C. Cerrotta IC8TEM, "IL F.A.I., quello strano fenomeno del dopo E-sporadico. Analisi del modo di propagazione ed esposizione di osservazioni in merito", Capri, May 2011 (in Italian). Cone geometry, a1 + a2 = 180° rule, elevation formula, season and times, Geneva scatter point.
SRC|2. G. Cerrotta IC8FAX, "Field Aligned Irregularities", web page https://qsl.net/ic8fax/page4.html. Reflection cone and supplementary contours, known scatter points (Geneva, Brussels, Budapest, JN92, JN41/42), example elevations (IW1AZJ 35° at ~150 km, IC8CQF 6° at ~800 km), season May to mid-September, 16-22 UTC, squares worked from JN70CN, reference to K9SE's program.
SRC|3. F. Egano IK3XTV, «Propagazione sui 6 metri - Caratteristiche generali» (Propagation on 6 metres - General characteristics), Amateur Radio Propagation Studies, doc. n. 10.36.43, April 2003, https://www.qsl.net/ik3xtv/ARCHIVIO/PROPAGAZIONE%20SUI%206%20METRI.pdf : indicative distances of Es contacts on 6 metres - single hop minimum 500-650 km, optimum 1500-2000 km, maximum 2200-2400 km; double hop minimum 2800-3000 km, optimum 3200-4000 km, maximum 4400-5000 km; triple hop optimum 4800-6400 km, maximum 6900 km; skip shortening on 28 MHz (400-500 km) and on 50 MHz (500-700 km) as an indicator of the MUF; geographical selectivity, duration of openings, season and times. Other articles by IK3XTV at https://www.qsl.net/ik3xtv/ (e.g. "E sporadico alle medie latitudini", Radio Rivista, June 2012; "Prevedere l'E sporadico", with IZ3ESV).
SRC|4. C. Cerrotta IC8TEM, personal experience: in multi-hop the first hop can be short, within 1200 km of his QTH ("short first hop" hypothesis in the SPORADIC-E tab).
SRC|5. C. Cerrotta IC8TEM, G. Sartori IV3GTH, "METEOR-SCATTER. Ovvero come collegare l'Europa in VHF tutto l'anno senza tante pretese" (main source of YA-MS, METEOR SCATTER tab; the YA-MS sources are listed in the program itself).
SRC|22. F. Egano IK3XTV, "Propagazione sui 2 metri" (https://www.qsl.net/ik3xtv/ARCHIVIO/PROPAGAZIONE%20IN%20GAMMA%202%20METRI.pdf): very dense Es clouds are needed to bend grazing rays up to 200 MHz; 144 MHz is reached in about 1 % of the cases of Es usable on HF.
SRC|23. D. Whitehead, "Sporadic E - A Mystery Solved?", QST, October 1997 (https://www.arrl.org/files/file/Technology/tis/info/pdf/9710039.pdf): "The MUF for mid-latitude ES can reach 150 MHz and higher".
SRC|24. ARRL, "World Above 50 MHz Distance Records", update of 19 May 2023 (N. Stearns AA7A), https://www.arrl.org/files/file/WA50-Standings/Distance-Records-19-May-2023-Rev.pdf: sporadic-E records only on 50 and 144 MHz; no Es records on 222 and 432 MHz.
H1|F.A.I.
SRC|6. W. Rauh DL5MCG (with DL4MEA), "FAI & DB0FAI", https://qsl.net/dl5mcg/fai.htm. 144 MHz. Height ~110 km, aim at the scatter point and not at the DX station. Beacon DB0FAI (JN58IC, 144.490 MHz, 1000 W ERP, 16 elements beamed at 305°, active from May 1990 to 27 August 2014). A reception from Dresden, reported as aurora, with the antenna at 290°: the computed scatter point is over Great Britain, near Sheffield. DL4MEA-DL5MCG test with SV1DH in June-July 1987: 1571 km, contact on 10 days out of 15, scatter «over Budapest». Minimum equipment: a long yagi and at least 100 W (SV1DH was also worked with an 8-element and 50 W). Used to verify the calculation.

SRC|29. F. Egano IK3XTV, «Propagazione sui 10 metri - Caratteristiche generali» (Propagation on 10 metres - General characteristics), Amateur Radio Propagation Studies, https://www.qsl.net/ik3xtv/ARCHIVIO/PROPAGAZIONE%20SUI%2010%20METRI.pdf : Es clouds at a height of about 100 km, single hop limited to around 2000 km, double hops possible but rarer; skip under 400 km on 28 MHz as an indicator of a MUF above 50 MHz; foEs 7.2 MHz for 10 metres, 12-14 MHz for the upper VHF.
SRC|30. G. Jacobs W3ASK, S. Leinwoll, «VHF Ionospheric Propagation», CQ Magazine, November 1969 (https://wtfda.org/wp-content/uploads/mem/vhf_ionospheric_propagation.pdf): the sporadic-E MUF is derived from the minimum skip distance on 28 MHz; a skip under 500 miles on 28 MHz indicates that 50 MHz will open in the same direction; single Es hop up to about 1300 miles, double up to about 2400 miles.
SRC|31. P. Herrman N0NBH, solar data and VHF conditions (https://www.hamqsl.com/solarxml.php, banner https://www.hamqsl.com/solar101vhf.php; VHF data from DXrobot and Make More Miles on VHF): E-skip Europe and North America, aurora, solar and geomagnetic indices; to be downloaded at most once an hour.
SRC|32. Make More Miles on VHF (https://mmmonvhf.de/ms.php): chart of today's meteor activity (https://mmmonvhf.de/ms/ms.png) with data from the Radio Meteor Observing Bulletin (RMOB), updated every hour.
SRC|33. G0ISW, «VHF DX Resource» (https://www.qsl.net/g0isw/g0iswmain.html): aurora on 50 and 144 MHz (50 MHz favoured, 432 MHz very difficult because of the Doppler); Kp of 5 or more; antennas between north and east; distances 250-1100 km; auroral E develops after the aurora, without Doppler.
SRC|34. K7CW and VE7DAY, PNWVHFS group, 2022 (https://groups.google.com/g/pnwvhfs/c/x7-pBO19WGc): aurora on 50 MHz between the north-western United States and Canada, heading 43°, 337 km; FT8 does not decode, Q65 with wide submodes recommended.
SRC|35. E. Pocock W3EP, «Sporadic-E propagation at VHF: a review of progress and prospects», QST, April 1988 (reprint in UKSMG Six News: https://uksmg.org/content/sporade.htm): 50, 144 and 220 MHz, North America; MUF about 5.3 times the foEs; maximum single hop about 2200 km with the layer at 105 km; with a cloud that brings the MUF to 144 MHz the skip on 50 MHz is about 650 km; Es on 144 MHz is less than a tenth of that on 50 MHz; F.A.I. up to 2000 km on 144 MHz.
SRC|36. P. Dyer WA5IYX, «A Seven Year Study of 50 MHz Sporadic-E Propagation», CQ, August 1972 (https://www.qsl.net/p/pjdyer/cq/cq7208a.htm): 50 MHz, Texas 1964-1970; almost 80% of openings from May to August; peaks at 10-12 and 18-20 local time; single hop up to about 2250 km, double up to about 4000 km; minimum observed distance about 450 km.
SRC|37. J. Dzekevich K1YOW, «Upper-Level Lows and 6-Meter Sporadic E», QST, December 2017 (https://hamsci.org/sites/default/files/publications/2017_QST_Dzekevich_UpperLevelLows.pdf): 50 MHz, North America; Es layers at 90-120 km, 0.5-5 km thick, 10-1000 km wide; correlation with upper-level meteorological lows.
SRC|38. J. Dzekevich K1YOW, «Winter Sporadic-E-Like Propagation on 6 Meters», CQ, November 2020 (abstract: https://hamsci.org/bibcite/reference/173): 50 MHz, winter sporadic E and the jet stream.
SRC|39. C. Luetzelschwab K9LA, «Interesting Propagation on 6 Meters», 6m BBQ 2018 (https://contestuniversity.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/5.-6m-BBQ-K9LA-Austin-2018.pdf): 50 MHz; FT8 decodes with a MUF of at least about 44 MHz, CW with about 45.5 MHz.
SRC|40. R. Harrison VK2ZRH, «A New Model of VHF Sporadic E Propagation», DUBUS 2/2011 (https://vk4ghz.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=17446): 50 and 144 MHz, Australia; with the «chordal hop» model (rippled Es) a foEs of 12-17 MHz is enough for 144 MHz and the MUF almost doubles compared with the classical secant law.
SRC|41. EI7GL, VK-ZL openings on 144 MHz (https://ei7gl.blogspot.com/2019/12/recent-3000km-opening-on-144-mhz.html ; https://ei7gl.blogspot.com/2022/01/3155-km-opening-on-144-mhz-between.html): 144 MHz, Australia and New Zealand; beyond 3100 km the path is explained by sporadic E followed by a tropospheric duct over the sea, not by a double hop.
SRC|42. S. Franke K9AN, J. Taylor K1JT, «The MSK144 Protocol for Meteor-Scatter Communication», QEX, September/October 2017 (https://ARRL.org/files/file/QEX_Next_Issue/SeptOct2017/FrankeTaylor.pdf): 50 and 144 MHz; useful pings above about 70 ms; on 144 MHz pings last about 1/8 as long as on 50 MHz.
SRC|43. J. Taylor K1JT, «WSJT: New Software for VHF Meteor-Scatter Communication», QST, December 2001 (https://mirror.unpad.ac.id/orari/orari-diklat/teknik/jt65/docs/WSJT_QST_Dec2001.pdf): 50 and 144 MHz; distances 800-1800 km; median QSO duration 5 minutes on 6 m and 13 minutes on 2 m.
SRC|44. Bureau of Meteorology, Australian Space Weather Forecasting Centre, Space Weather API (https://sws-data.sws.bom.gov.au/ ; specification: https://sws-data.sws.bom.gov.au/api-docs): Australian K index, Dst, alerts, watches and outlooks of the southern aurora (k_aus, latitude band); POST requests in JSON with a free key.
SRC|45. World Data Center for Geomagnetism, Kyoto, real-time AE index (https://wdc.kugi.kyoto-u.ac.jp/ae_realtime/): auroral electrojet activity; non-commercial use only. The digital files stop at October 2026: the program opens the page in the browser.
SRC|46. World Data Center for Geomagnetism, Kyoto, hourly quicklook Dst index (https://wdc.kugi.kyoto-u.ac.jp/dst_realtime/presentmonth/index.html): intensity of geomagnetic storms; non-commercial use only.
SRC|47. SuperDARN Canada, real-time convection (https://superdarn.ca/realtime-convection): northern-hemisphere convection map with the equatorward boundary (Heppner-Maynard) and the OVATION oval; © SuperDARN Canada, link only.
SRC|48. Ch. Newton G2FKZ, «Radio Auroras», RSGB, 1991 (review by W. Reeve: https://www.reeve.com/Documents/Book%20Reviews/Reeve_BookReview_RadioAuroras.pdf): aurora mainly on 144 MHz; maximum distances around 1000 km north-south and 2000 km east-west; peak activity 1.5-2 years after solar maximum.
SRC|49. P. Kennewell, P. Wilkinson, «Transequatorial Radio Propagation», IPS Radio and Space Services, Australia (https://www.sws.bom.gov.au/Category/Educational/Other%20Topics/Radio%20Communication/Transequatorial.pdf): afternoon TEP (15-19 local time, 5000-6500 km, up to about 60 MHz) and evening TEP (20-23 local time, 3000-8000 km, up to 144 MHz); equatorial anomaly crests at ±10-20° magnetic latitude; path symmetrical about the magnetic equator and within about 15° of the magnetic north-south direction.
SRC|50. DK3XT, Make More Miles on VHF, database of auroral openings 1991-2026 (https://www.mmmonvhf.de/au.php): aurora QSOs with time, callsigns, locators, beam headings and reports, on 144 and 432 MHz in Europe.
SRC|51. F. Egano IK3XTV, T. de Longhi IZ3ESV, «Prevedere l'E sporadico», Amateur Radio Propagation Studies (https://www.qsl.net/ik3xtv/ARCHIVIO/PREVEDERE%20L%27E%20SPORADICO.pdf): 50 and 144 MHz; sporadic E occurs in more than 90% of cases with a quiet geomagnetic field (Kp ≤ 3, Ap ≤ 17); openings up to 144 MHz are about 5% of the total. The program flags it on the SPORADIC-E map as a simple warning when the Kp exceeds 3: on the author's 50 MHz log the frequency of Es does not decrease with Kp > 3.
SRC|52. W. Reeve, «HF Aurora Reflections Observed at Anchorage, Alaska», 2022 (https://www.reeve.com/Documents/Articles%20Papers/Reeve_AuroraRadioObsrv.pdf): observations at 15 MHz from a single station, Alaska, without counts; most of the observed auroral reflections fall within about 4 hours of local solar midnight, while the studies above 30 MHz cited by the author put the peaks before midnight; reflection regions 500-1000 km to the north. The program uses the 4-hour window as a time-of-day weight in the AURORA tab.
SRC|53. ARRL, «Strong geomagnetic storm enlivens the Magic Band», 2013 (https://www.arrl.org/news/strong-geomagnetic-storm-enlivens-the-magic-band): aurora on 50 MHz in the United States (Pennsylvania, Iowa) with K index 5-7 and a G3 storm; antennas to the north; CW contacts with OH and ES.
SRC|54. SM2CEW, SM0FOB, «Sporadic E and Auroral E in the Arctic», 2021 (https://sm0fobradio.blogspot.com/2021/03/sporadic-e-and-auroral-e-in-arctic.html): 50 and 144 MHz, Arctic Sweden; auroral E comes after the aurora with weaker and steadier signals, by forward scatter on the direct path (aurora instead requires off-axis beam heading); more frequent in summer and near solar maximum.
SRC|55. ANVDG, IPS Aurora Sightings, Australia, November 2004 (https://listserver.ips.gov.au/pipermail/ips-aurora-sightings/2004-November/000181.html): southern aurora; on 50 MHz contacts up to about 2000 km on east-west paths, on 144 MHz the north-south path is at the limit; antennas to the south.
SRC|56. EI7GL, «11460km contact on 50 MHz between the USA & Saudi Arabia - 2nd July 2020», March 2021 (https://ei7gl.blogspot.com/2021/03/11460km-contact-on-50-mhz-between-usa.html): 50 MHz, HZ1SK-K4MOG in FT8, 11460 km, sporadic E with at least six hops; a few weeks earlier K1TOL-7Z1SJ, 9933 km, about five hops. The program uses it only as a statistical note: an alert for 50 MHz QSOs beyond 11000 km.
SRC|57. E. Miller K8GU, «Aurora 15 July 2012», AmateurRadio.com, 16 July 2012 (https://www.amateurradio.com/aurora-15-july-2012/): 144 MHz, United States east coast; K8GU (FM19LA) - KA1ZE/3 (FN01XT), 322 km; aurora beam heading 45° against 345° for the direct path; Doppler of about 300 Hz downwards. Discarded as a test case by the author, because the antenna used is not known: it remains as a citation.
SRC|58. EI7GL, «50 MHz opening between Guam and Europe», June 2021 (https://ei7gl.blogspot.com/2021/06/50-mhz-opening-between-guam-and-europe.html): 50 MHz, Europe - Guam (KG6DX) on 1 June 2021, 11000-12190 km in FT8, probably sporadic E with about 6 hops, preceded by a week of Europe - Japan openings around 9000 km. It is the basis of the multi-hop alerts from precursors.
SRC|59. GM4FVM, «Multi-hop sporadic E opens up again», May 2025 (https://gm4fvm.blogspot.com/2025/05/multi-hop-sporadic-e-opens-up-again.html): 50 MHz, Scotland (IO85) - ZD7BG St Helena, 7922 km, sudden appearance and disappearance. The author attributes the QSO to a multi-hop Es of about four hops and considers TEP less likely; the hypothesis of TEP extended by Es comes from a commenter (OZ9QV). The program's calculation places the midpoint in the TEP band (inclination 19.7°) and reads it as sporadic E coupling into TEP: this is an interpretation by the program, different from the author's. The June openings towards Japan and China are described as multi-hop Es.
SRC|60. R. Takata, «The observation of Sporadic E using FT8 in JAPAN», HamSCI Workshop 2026 (https://hamsci.org/bibcite/reference/533): 50 MHz, Japan; from FT8 reports, with the midpoint as reflection point, Es structures turn out to be elongated east-west or slightly tilted, never purely north-south, in agreement with GNSS measurements. The program derives from it the choice of transverse paths for the multi-hop alerts.
SRC|61. R. Hardenberg PE1ITR, «Q65 and Aurora Propagation», 2 June 2024 (https://pe1itr.com/144mhz/144mhz-aurora-propagation-20240511.htm): 144 MHz, Netherlands, aurora of 11 May 2024 (recordings 15:00-15:30 UTC, 9-element Yagi and RTL-SDR); Q65-30D decodes the auroral signals, almost invisible on the WSJT-X waterfall but clearly visible on a wideband SDR waterfall; the signal is broadened and shifted by the Doppler effect, so RIT or split is needed. Cited in the mode advice of the AURORA tab.
SRC|62. EI5FK (IO51RT, Ireland), aurora logs on 144 and 432 MHz 2003-2005 (https://www.qsl.net/ei5fk/2005.htm and linked pages: 432au, 25july, 27july, 2maurlog, wkd, aug): 144 MHz (and 432 MHz on 20/11/2003), CW and SSB, 287 QSOs with time and locator, up to 2101 km (OH1NOR). The beam heading is not given. Data of the BENCHMARK AURORA test bench.
SRC|63. PA0O (JO33HG, Netherlands), map of the aurora QSOs on 144 MHz from the Log Analyzer program by DL4MFM, sent by the author: 29 correspondents with locator towards Germany, the Czech Republic, Austria, Switzerland, northern Italy, Slovenia, Croatia, Hungary and Romania (date not given). Data of the BENCHMARK AURORA test bench.
SRC|64. Q. Tang, H. Sun, Z. Du, J. Zhao, Y. Liu, Z. Zhao, X. Feng, «Unusual Enhancement of Midlatitude Sporadic-E Layers in Response to a Minor Geomagnetic Storm», Atmosphere 13 (2022) 816 (https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4433/13/5/816): East Asian ionosondes; in the minor geomagnetic storm of July 2013 foEs increases by 3-10 MHz at mid latitudes. It summarises a conflicting literature: Es increases with Kp (Zhou et al.), does not depend on geomagnetic activity (Pietrella and Bianchi), responds one way or the other (Maksyutin and Sherstyukov).
SRC|65. G0ISW, «Propagation identification» (V3.42, October 2024, https://www.qsl.net/g0isw/g0iswpropident.htm): 50, 70 and 144 MHz, United Kingdom; distances by propagation mode: shortest sporadic-E beyond 400 km on 50 and 70 MHz, beyond 1400 km on 144 MHz (single hop 1400-2400 km, double up to 3200 km). The program compares it with the author's minimum distances (450 km on 50 and 70 MHz, 900 km with confirmation on 144 MHz).
SRC|66. R. G. Cracknell G2AHU, «Equatorial Propagation», UKSMG archive (https://www.uksmg.org/content/equitorialpropagation.htm), with the observations of ZE2JV (QST, December 1958), the Salisbury report (May 1960) and QST November-December 1981: 50 MHz (tests on 144 MHz without success beyond northern Italy), Southern Africa and Europe; high-density TEP zones at about 5-15° from the zero-dip line, north-south contacts around 6000 km, best at the equinoxes and less reliable at the solar cycle minimum. The program takes its 15° value for the band of the trans-equatorial rule, applied to the inclination (author's choice). Similarities: like Kennewell and Wilkinson [49], TEP perpendicular to the magnetic equator and symmetrical. Differences: Cracknell gives 5-15° of latitude, [49] crests at ±10-20° of magnetic latitude.
SRC|67. A. T. Chartier, T. R. Hanley, D. J. Emmons, «Long-distance propagation of 162 MHz shipping information links associated with sporadic E», Atmospheric Measurement Techniques 15 (2022) 6387-6393 (https://doi.org/10.5194/amt-15-6387-2022): AIS signals from ships at 162 MHz received beyond 1000 km by the US Coast Guard network (eastern United States and Puerto Rico, 13-15 July 2021); inferred foEs of at least 27 MHz, against the usual 6-9 MHz, confirmed by Digisonde ionosondes saturated in the daytime and by COSMIC-2 radio-occultation maps. It concerns 162 MHz, not 50 or 144 MHz; the AIS data are not public. Source approved by the author on 14/11/2026.
SRC|68. B. W. Reinisch, I. A. Galkin, «Global Ionospheric Radio Observatory (GIRO)», Earth, Planets and Space 63 (2011) 377-381 (https://doi.org/10.5047/eps.2011.03.001): worldwide network of Digisonde ionosondes with automatic reading of the ionograms (ARTIST); data from the lgdc.uml.edu/fastchar/getbest service. List of the stations: ULCAR, University of Massachusetts Lowell (https://ulcar.uml.edu/stationlist.html). The GIRO data are distributed under the CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 licence: non-commercial use, citation of the source, same licence for redistributed data; the program is free and cites GIRO in the tab. Used by the IONOSONDES sub-tab (foEs, foF2, MUF(D)). Source approved by the author on 14/11/2026.
SRC|69. DM6AX, «MUF Muncher» (https://github.com/mooxle/Muf_Muncher, MIT licence): program that reads foEs and MUF live from the GIRO ionosondes. The program takes from it only the idea and the address of the GIRO service, not the code. Source approved by the author on 14/11/2026.
SRC|70. A. T. Chartier, «sporadice» repository (https://github.com/alexchartier/sporadice, script analyze_dx_data.m, no licence stated): method of the article [67] applied to the 28 and 50 MHz spots of 11 May 2024 (00-04 UTC): midpoint of each link, height 100 km, minimum foEs with the secant law, NmE = foEs²/80.6, 2° x 2° grid in 5-minute steps, for each cell the highest value. The program describes the method and redoes it with its own model (reflection points of all hops); the code is not copied. Used by the Es CLOUDS OVER TIME sub-tab. Source approved by the author on 14/11/2026.
SRC|71. V. Grassmann DF5AI, «Hidden Mode of Sporadic-E?», UKSMG, 1999 (https://www.uksmg.org/content/moremagicon6.htm), 50 MHz (144 MHz only mentioned): 979 contacts from 1983 to 1999; short-hop mode with the peak around 950 km and the multiples at 1800-1900 and 2700-2800 km, long-hop mode with the peak at 1500-1600 km and the double at 3000-3200 km; for the short hop he assumes a lower reflection (80-90 km). Comparison: it confirms the minima of IK3XTV [3] (nothing under 500 km, very little under 700 km) and the short-first-hop experience of IC8TEM [4]; its optima (1500-1600 km) are shorter than those of IK3XTV (1500-2000 km). Used for the multi-hop chains with short hops at the ends. Source approved by the author on 20/11/2026.
SRC|72. EI7GL, posts on UN7MBH (Kazakhstan), 70 MHz, 2019 and 2021 (https://ei7gl.blogspot.com/search/label/UN7MBH): UN7MBH with Spain at 4300-4600 km and with the Canary Islands at 6112 km, read as triple hop. Comparison: double and triple hop distances of IK3XTV [3]; it is 70 MHz and not 50, and the number of hops is inferred from the distance. Used as an example of a chain with the end in Central Asia.
